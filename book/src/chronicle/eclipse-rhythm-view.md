# Eclipse Rhythm and View

Eclipse Seasons began with dated solar and lunar events. Eclipse Rhythm and
View makes their structure observable: every moon's bounded recurrence ladder,
the three-return exeligmos and its surface closure, the season parade, solar
ground-track regions, and the sight available to a placed observer.

The physical derivation stays small and explicit. Existing moon-node facts and
closed-form syzygies provide the events. Recurrence is a derived description
of those facts, not an Earth claim: generated worlds may have a useful
world-specific return without carrying Earth's literal Saros. The exeligmos
keeps terrestrial longitude closure separate from orbital node-phase slip, so
the two kinds of rhythm cannot be mistaken for one another.

The observer view distinguishes a geographic region from a person's result.
A solar event carries its approximate swept band and directed longitude
coverage; a lunar event carries night-side visibility without a solar track.
Latitude bounds, longitude normalization, poles, retrograde rotation,
tidally locked worlds, and long crossings are explicit. The producer decides
the sight tier; clients receive that decision through `scene/eclipses/v3`.

The v3 scene document uses snapped integer tick bounds, structured recurrence,
optional observer input, event ordering, multi-moon coincidence summaries,
and deterministic native/WASM output. The almanac and reference page use the
same vocabulary. No new genesis draw, save fact, epoch, or census metric was
needed, and no N-body dynamics entered the model.

The campaign leaves partiality grading, animated shadows, lunar shading,
standstills, transits and occultations, tidal braking, variable stars,
equation-of-time work, aurorae, and cultural interpretation to later work.
