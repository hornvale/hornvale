# The Coda

The Coda did not ship a simulation feature. It made the campaign record
legible as a whole without mistaking a finished record for expendable clutter.
The audit preserves every specification, plan, chronicle, and retrospective
at its existing path; the new reconciliation table is a shelf-mark over those
records, not an archive.

At close preparation, the table covers 1,325 primary records in its four
directories. Of those, 907 are `shipped` and 418 are `active`; no row is
`partial`, `superseded`, `abandoned`, or `unresolved`. The four Coda records
remain active while this package awaits G6. The active rows are not a backlog
invented by the audit: each is an existing campaign record whose closure was
not established by direct evidence.

The guard is deliberately narrower than a story about matching names. It
parses the committed table, requires each primary path exactly once, checks
the closed disposition and residue vocabulary, and verifies registry targets
when a row names one. A source-to-chronicle relationship is accepted only
when its own text declares the closure or both records carry an exact campaign
identifier; a filename resemblance is only a lead.

The audit added no idea-registry rows and deduplicated none. It found no
supported durable product residue to promote: ordinary process observations
remain in their retrospective or campaign record, rather than swelling the
registry into an issue tracker. Its result is conservative by design: retain
the evidence, name what is still active, and let a future campaign supply a
direct closure trail rather than rewriting history to make a table neater.
