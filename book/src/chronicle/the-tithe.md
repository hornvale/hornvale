# The Tithe

*Its predecessor measured conflict deeply sub-critical and named the term it
was missing. This campaign supplied that term, doubled the number, and
falsified the same wager a second time — and reading the final histogram
honestly requires knowing how many mechanisms were added chasing it.*

*The Tumult* built predation, measured it, and shipped a falsification: the
size distribution of cascading displacement is not a power law but a geometric
law with a hard cutoff, branching ratio **σ ≈ 0.051** against a critical value
of one. Its most useful product was not that number but the fact that the
campaign had **two** failed builds which fail from opposite sides. A crowding
sandpile had **drive without dissipation**, so every avalanche ran to the depth
cap. Predation has **dissipation without accumulation**: every hop of a chain
pays a war loss and a journey, every victim is by construction weaker than
whoever displaced it, and nothing is stored *between* relaxations whose release
could make a large event.

Self-organized criticality needs both terms. This campaign supplies the missing
one, and the question it was built to answer was preregistered before any of it
existed: **does accumulation move the branching ratio off 0.051?**

It does. It roughly doubles it. And the distribution's shape does not change at
all.

## The prize decides

The accumulating structure had to be a **standing relation**, and the design
question was what makes a raid end in one rather than in an eviction. The
answer needed no new decision variable, because the goods already differ: a
raid's outcome follows the **mobility of its prize**.

A cell — land, a mine, a forage ground — is a perfectly rival *immobile* good.
It is takeable only by occupying it, so contact over it is necessarily
conquest: taking is evicting, which is the shipped path and is unchanged. But a
community's own people and their product are *mobile*, and a mobile prize can
be taken again next year without displacing anybody. So a raider that beats its
target by the same margin as before now resolves two outcomes rather than one.
If the target's cell is worth more, seize it. If the target's cell is no better
but the target is **productive** — it has growth headroom, so there is a
surplus to take — **subordinate** it: the loser keeps its ground and begins
paying tribute.

The second branch is genuine new motive rather than a relabelling, and this is
checkable rather than rhetorical: the shipped covetousness gate refuses outright
to raid a poorer neighbour. Under tribute, a neighbour whose *land* is no prize
but whose *people* are productive becomes worth milking. That is the
accumulation term in one sentence — **the dominant grows without moving.**

An inherited claim had to be re-observed before any of this could be designed.
Both the predecessor's specification and its closing investigation asserted that
a standing inter-community relation "needs a persistent relation the ledger has
no shape for — a save-format change and a real new subsystem". That was simply
false, and it had been costing the project a mis-estimated price on the whole
direction. The ledger's fact envelope is already a typed, directed, dated
entity-to-entity edge, and the deep-history bake already emits two of them.
Tribute needed **one registered predicate**, `pays-tribute-to`, and nothing
else: no new fact shape, no new cause of ending, no new seeded draw, no new
crate.

## A negotiation nobody in this world is competent to conduct

The obvious mechanism is a fixed tribute rate, and a fixed rate is exactly
wrong — for the reason the crowding sandpile was wrong. A constant rate is an
*equilibrating* force, and an equilibrating force is what makes a build smooth
and raid-free. Nobody here runs double-entry accounting. Both sides are
guessing, and the subjugated withhold.

Lifted, that is the **principal-agent problem under asymmetric information** —
tax farming, sharecropping, the protection racket — and its historical result
hands over a deterministic mechanism at zero cost, because the asymmetry is
already structural in the bake. **The patron taxes what it can see; the vassal
holds what it has.** Those are two different numbers already: a cell's
era-effective carrying capacity, which is public, and a community's population,
which is not. Land tax has always been assessed on area and never on the
granary, for precisely this reason.

Three terms follow, and none of them draws from the seed:

- **assessment** — the patron's demand, set from the cell's visible capacity;
- **remittance** — what the vassal actually hands over, paid out of the epoch's
  growth increment and out of standing stock above a floor;
- **concealment** — the gap the vassal controls, derived from its authored
  in-group radius, so an insular people hides more from outsiders than an
  expansive one.

There is a pleasing symmetry in where those readings come from. The patron
reads a dimension of its own **mind**; the vassal reads a dimension of its own
**society**. Both sides of the negotiation consult a different axis of the same
authored psychology, and neither can see the other's.

Both errors destabilise, in opposite directions. Under-assess, and the vassal
accumulates until it can throw its patron off. Over-assess, and it is crushed.
Fixed points do exist — a concealment that exactly offsets an over-assessment
sits still — so the honest claim is that the system has no *attracting*
equilibrium, which is the thing a constant rate could never deliver.

## Where tribute lands, and why it is not people

A negation pass over the finished design found a defect that would have failed
the campaign for a purely mechanical reason and then blamed criticality for it.
If remittance is added to the patron's **population**, the patron starves. Its
cell's capacity is unchanged by conquest, so pressure — population times need,
over effective capacity — climbs until the collapse threshold kills it of
famine. **A successful extractor would eat itself**, and the readout would have
reported "accumulation does not chain" when the truth was that mass had been
added to a fixed container.

So tribute lands in a new per-community scalar: **wealth, not bodies**. It
feeds strength, so a patron that collects becomes harder to beat; it never
enters the pressure term, so it never eats. It decays slowly, so a hoard is not
immortal, and it is lost with the community when that community dies — a
dominant's fall releases what it held. Historically this is exact: tribute
becomes granaries, walls, and retainers, which is strength the local land does
not have to feed.

The store also generalises past this slice. The parent idea — one community's
product ending up with another — has four children: seizure, tribute, gift,
and **trade**. Trade needs the identical accumulator, so the store is the
shared prerequisite of the whole contact program rather than a local
convenience.

## One value, 2258 times

Halfway through, the mechanism was built, the loop closed, and the numbers were
bad. An investigation was budgeted rather than a recalibration, and it found
something far more interesting than the defect it went looking for.

Across twenty-four worlds, the ratio of a relation's opening assessment to the
vassal cell's capacity took **exactly one value — 0.025 — across all 2258
relations.** Not a narrow distribution: one value. There was no patron-side
term anywhere in the tribute rule. The only per-people input was the *vassal's*
concealment, and every patron in the world took the whole growth increment
forever.

The consequence is worth stating in the strongest available form, because it is
what made the fix inevitable. A Sopranos bust-out and a Roman census were not
merely the same code path. **Nothing in the model could have distinguished them
even in principle.**

The same investigation explained the collapse it had been called to diagnose,
and overturned the explanation that had been offered for it. Two attractors
already existed, selected by a vassal's size at the moment it was subordinated:
above a root at roughly 0.146 of capacity a relation was sustainable, and below
it the vassal crashed to the floor in a median two epochs and froze there —
79.2% of relations. The modal vassal is a fresh daughter settlement, which is
always below that root. **It was doomed at conception.** And the world had not
been thinned by fertility at all: farmed vassals died young, so their sites
stopped producing daughters, and the count of community-epochs fell while the
per-eligible founding rate barely moved. Most sharply of all, 567 of 710
depth-zero losses were bled vassals annihilated on being raided: **the bleeding
did not reduce conflict, it made conflict terminal, and a cascade needs
survivors to roll.**

## The horizon is a discount rate

Two ideonomy passes on "there is no patron-side term" landed on
renewable-resource economics, and the mapping is exact rather than analogical,
because the bake already grows communities logistically. A logistic increment
is maximised at **half of capacity**. That is maximum sustainable yield, and it
is the peak of a curve the bake already computes every epoch.

A patron maximising the **discounted future stream** holds its vassal *at* that
peak. A patron maximising **this epoch** strips the stock. Same rule, one
parameter — and the parameter was already authored, already spread across the
peoples, and entirely unread by the simulation: **the mind's time horizon**,
immediate at zero and generational at one. It becomes the discount rate, and it
sets a setpoint: a target stock interpolating between the bare survival floor
and half of capacity.

Everything the campaign had been trying to enumerate now falls out of one
number. The Danegeld, the protection racket, tax farming, the imperial census,
and the bust-out are the **same rule at different horizons** — a family that is
generated, not listed. The health feedback built earlier stops being a loop
hunting around whatever happened and becomes the *controller* that reaches the
setpoint, correcting for the population the patron cannot see. And extinction
arrives as **Clark's case**: in resource economics, extermination is optimal
when the discount rate exceeds the resource's intrinsic growth rate. A
subjugated people can therefore still be destroyed — but only by the
shortest-sighted patrons, as a consequence of the same optimisation rather than
as a special rule.

Two refinements followed from the same idea and were measured separately. A
patron with foresight **declines a vassal too small to farm**, which took
relations opening below the patron's own viability threshold from 20.3% to
zero; the short-horizon peoples are *specified* to keep taking doomed vassals,
so the absolute share of below-root relations barely moved, and that is the
design rather than a miss. And a patron holding many vassals treats each as
more expendable: its **effective** horizon shortens hyperbolically with its
holdings, which raises extraction 58% across the first three vassals where the
per-people rule alone was flat. The obvious linear form was rejected because it
goes negative past a few holdings — an extermination order at the top of the
distribution.

## The subjugated answer back

Concealment is a passive response. Two active ones were added, both total
functions of frozen state with no agent decision and no new draw. A vassal
whose burden exceeds a threshold **flees**, walking off its cell rather than
continue in that condition. A vassal that comes to out-muscle its patron by the
dominance margin **revolts**, and the relation dissolves.

Revolt was the avalanche trigger the headline metric had never had. It fired
**zero times in thirty worlds**, and the cause was structural rather than a
mis-set threshold. The largest strength ratio any relation ever reached was
**1.029** against the 1.5 the rule requires. Forming a relation needs a 1.5×
advantage, so reversing one needs a 2.25× swing; the patron's store then widens
the gap monotonically; and every path that damages a patron **kills it
outright**, because a patron that loses a raid closes its record and closure
dissolved its whole portfolio. **A patron was never wounded, only healthy or
dead** — so the state revolt requires did not exist.

The fix is one rule. A community that relocates — closing here and reopening
there — **keeps its tribute relations**; only a community that genuinely dies
loses them. And the continuity is **role-asymmetric**, which is the part worth
keeping. A community keeps the relations it holds **as patron** — a lord's
claim travels with him — but drops the one it owes **as subordinate**: a vassal
that flees is gone.

That asymmetry was not a guess. The role-blind version shipped first and was
measured: an obligation that follows the runaway turns flight into a change of
address rather than an escape, and produces **repeat leavers** — the same
communities fleeing again every epoch because fleeing discharged nothing.
Making it asymmetric cut pooled flights from 647 to 83 and repeat leavers from
185 to 17, a 91% drop, and flight became a genuine exit. It is also defensible
on realism alone, independently of any metric: a defeated lord who flees to
another holding still holds his vassals' obligation; what he has lost is the
strength to enforce it. A defeated empire that shrinks is more plausible than
one that evaporates.

**This mechanism's predictions were written down before its code existed.** By
this point the campaign had amended its own specification four times, three of
them following a disappointing measurement, and the cumulative shape of that is
metric-chasing whatever each local justification. So the fifth amendment states
in advance what it expects, with both branches made informative:

1. **Revolts will fire.** If they remain at zero, the wounded-patron diagnosis
   is wrong.
2. **The cascade distribution is the open question.** A move toward a heavy tail
   confirms the campaign's thesis. **Revolts firing while the shape stays
   geometric is a *stronger* falsification than the current null** — it would
   show that even accumulation which can fail does not self-organize here.
3. **No constant is tuned toward either outcome**, and this is the last
   mechanism. Whatever is measured afterward is the answer.

All three adjudicated. **Prediction 1 confirmed**: the maximum strength ratio
went from 1.029 to **4.95**, and across 66,519 observations there were exactly
27 threshold crossings and exactly 27 revolts — none missed, none
double-counted. **Prediction 3 confirmed**: the only constants in the change
are inside a test fixture. **Prediction 2 was falsified, on the branch the
specification itself had designated the stronger falsification.**

## What the world did

Pooled over the same thirty seeds the predecessor pooled, at 7183 conquests,
the cascade-size histogram is `[634, 36, 0×10]` — 634 cascades of size one, 36
of size two or three, and nothing at all above size three. Replicated over a
hundred seeds and 22,255 conquests: `[1889, 98, 0×10]`, the same shape at three
times the sample.

**Accumulation moved the branching ratio, and moved it by the same factor on
both samples.**

| build | sample | histogram | conquests | **σ** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Tumult | 1..=30 | `[38, 2]` | 886 | **0.050–0.052** |
| The Tumult | 1..=100 | `[138, 3]` | 2974 | **0.051–0.052** |
| **The Tithe** | 1..=30 | `[634, 36]` | 7183 | **0.109–0.115** |
| **The Tithe** | 1..=100 | `[1889, 98]` | 22255 | **0.103–0.109** |

σ is reported as an interval because the histogram is log-binned: the second
bin spans sizes two and three, so the secondary count is bounded rather than
known. The preregistered question therefore has a two-part answer and both
parts ship. Accumulation **did** move the branching ratio off 0.051 — it
roughly doubled it. But σ ≈ 0.1 is not σ ≈ 1, and the distribution's *shape*
did not change in any respect:

- **Occupied support is still the first two bins.** The largest cascade in a
  hundred worlds is three displacements — **0.48 decades** of support, against
  the ≥ 1.5 decades a power-law claim requires to be well-posed.
- **The per-octave drop did not soften into a tail.** The first bin falls to
  the second by **17.6×** over thirty seeds and 19.3× over a hundred, where a
  heavy tail falls by two- to fourfold. The predecessor's drop was 19× and 46×;
  the softening is the same factor of two σ shows, not a change of family.
- **The third bin and above are empty on every one of the hundred worlds** —
  not one cascade past three displacements in roughly twenty-two thousand
  conquests. A geometric law with σ ≈ 0.1 predicts exactly that. A power law
  does not.

Seed forty-two, the world that never crowds, now resolves **591 conquests**
against the predecessor's 76, opens 1776 occupation records, and ends with 329
communities alive — a fuller world, not a thinned one. It forms 635 tribute
relations of which 159 still stand at the end, collects 7349 units of tribute
across 4487 collections, and its largest holding is five vassals. Sixty-eight
vassals flee and one revolts.

So this is the second documented falsification of the criticality wager, at a
doubled branching ratio: geometric with a hard cutoff, deep in the sub-critical
regime. **The diagnosis it leaves is structural, and both remaining levers were
deferred on purpose.** A revolt frees exactly one vassal — collapse-release,
where a fallen patron's whole network is freed at once, was a stated non-goal.
And the relation graph is a set of **one-level stars**: a vassal may not itself
take a vassal, and depth was the other stated non-goal. **A patron's failure
therefore has no medium along which to propagate.** An avalanche needs a
medium, and this slice has none. Accumulation alone is not enough, and depth or
release is the next lever — which is now an argument with a measurement behind
it rather than a speculation.

## The strategy family — what this campaign can claim without qualification

The variety criterion was written into the specification precisely because the
pre-amendment state had failed it so completely, and it is met unambiguously.
Where the extraction ratio had taken exactly one value across 2258 relations,
the shipped rule produces **3173 distinct relation-level extraction rates**
over the relations that ever collect, and 41,635 distinct per-collection rates
over 52,230 collections — and the variation is not noise, because it is
**monotone in the authored horizon on every axis**:

| patron people | time horizon | relations | extraction rate (median) | relation lifetime (median) | extinction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bugbear | 0.3 | 2032 | 0.1053 | 100 yr | **0.79%** |
| hobgoblin | 0.5 | 1843 | 0.0577 | 150 yr | 0.27% |
| kobold | 0.8 | 1526 | 0.0342 | 175 yr | **0.20%** |

A 3.1× spread in extraction rate, and lifetime rising with horizon exactly as
the discount model says it must. **Extinction is the exception and it
concentrates on the short horizon**, which is the criterion's own red line:
0.44% of relations end with the vassal's line ending, and a short-horizon
patron's vassal is **four times** likelier to be one of them than a
generational patron's. That is Clark's case arriving as a consequence of the
model rather than as an authored special case.

The table above is dated: it was measured before the campaign took in the rest
of the project's work, and that absorption is itself part of the result. It
brought a **fifth settled people, the gnoll**, whose authored time horizon is
**0.2** — shorter than any of the three above, and therefore the most
extractive patron in a strategy family that had been designed and measured
without it. The observable projection of the table, which the world carries
without any probe, now reads gnoll and bugbear at a median standing-relation
age of 150 days, hobgoblin at 225, and kobold at 275: the ordering holds, with
a new most-rapacious member at the top of it. More peoples means more
communities, more conquests, and far more flight, and every count in the
campaign roughly doubled with the map's occupancy. **The verdict did not change
in any particular; only the magnitudes moved, and they moved together.**

## The cycle that is not there

One secondary claim was carried on its own axis and adjudicated separately,
because bundling it into the primary verdict would have been dishonest in both
directions. The adaptive demand was made two-signed specifically so that it
*could* oscillate, reproducing the Ibn Khaldun secular cycle as a consequence
rather than as an authored feature — feedback with delay, the delay being the
epoch step.

There is no cycle. Over 754 relations holding at least twenty contiguous
collections, the dominant Fourier component of the detrended per-relation
remittance series sits at harmonic index one in **91.6%** of relations. Index
one *is* the series' own length — the lowest resolvable frequency — so what the
periodogram finds is **drift, not oscillation**: no period shorter than a
relation's whole life is resolvable in it. The world-level series behaves
identically, peaking at the first or second harmonic of a seventy-seven-epoch
span on twenty-six of thirty worlds, with no repeating peak in the
autocorrelation. The peak carries more power than a white-noise shuffle, but so
does any smooth series; against a red-noise null fitted to each relation's own
lag-one correlation the excess is small and concentrated in the trend.

The mechanism-level reading is not a surprise, and it is a real cost of the
discount-rate amendment. Giving the feedback a **setpoint** turned it from a
loop hunting around whatever happened into a **regulator steering toward a
target**, and a well-damped regulator converges — it does not oscillate. The
fourth amendment suppressed the very oscillation the third had made possible.
Measured on the per-relation series rather than on raw tribute volume, which
would have reported the population's shape rather than the demand's.

## Five amendments, and what protects the reader from them

This campaign amended its own specification **five times, four of them
following a disappointing measurement**. In order: the adaptive-demand rule was
corrected before implementation because the first formulation was a one-signed
ratchet that could not oscillate at all; hysteresis was added to patronage
transfer after roughly 87% of formations were measured to be rival patrons
swapping the same targets back and forth; the milk-never-kill cap was replaced
by a bleed once the health signal was shown structurally unable to go negative;
extraction became a discount-rate family after the ratio was measured at one
value across 2258 relations; and relation continuity was added after revolt was
measured to fire zero times.

Each has a local justification and several of them are among the campaign's
best work. **The cumulative shape is nonetheless metric-chasing**, and saying
so is not a rhetorical concession — it is the only thing that makes the final
histogram readable. It was named to the project's owner while the campaign was
still running, and he chose to proceed. The protection taken was to
**preregister the fifth amendment's predictions before any of its code
existed**, with both branches made informative in advance, so that the readout
confirms or falsifies a *prediction* rather than reporting a number and then
explaining it.

**A reader who sees only the final histogram, without knowing how many
mechanisms were added chasing it, has been misled.** No constant was tuned
toward either verdict at any point, and every floor the campaign committed sits
clear of its measured value rather than at it — but those are guarantees about
tuning, and the amendments were not tuning. They were mechanisms. The
disclosure is the guarantee.

## What this campaign is, and is not

It ships a standing inter-community relation over the ledger's existing fact
shape and one new predicate; subordination as the mobile-prize branch of the
raid rule; a negotiation whose asymmetry is structural rather than authored; a
store of wealth that feeds strength and never eats; extraction strategy as a
generated discount-rate family indexed by a psychology scalar that had been
authored and never read; horizon-aware subordination and the portfolio effect;
flight and revolt; role-asymmetric relation continuity across relocation; and a
second measured, published falsification of the criticality wager, at a
branching ratio that doubled.

It does **not** ship protection — a patron shielding its vassals from
third-party raids, and the over-reach that creates — nor chained tribute, where
a vassal's vassal remits upward, nor collapse-release as an avalanche. Those
three were deferred by name before the measurement, and the measurement is now
the argument for promoting two of them: depth is what makes an avalanche large,
and release is what makes a structure's failure propagate. Nor does it ship
assessment that goes **stale** — the informational asymmetry is fictional at
the moment of conquest, since the dominance test has just measured the target's
strength, so the stronger model is information fresh at conquest and decaying
with each epoch since enforcement — nor assessment *competence* varying by
people, which would restore the symmetry concealment currently enjoys alone.

Conflict in Hornvale now accumulates. It concentrates value into a structure
that can be inherited, fled, and overthrown, and whose severity is a
consequence of how far ahead its holder thinks. It is still sub-critical, and
the reason is no longer that nothing is stored. It is that nothing conducts.
