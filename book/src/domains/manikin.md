# The Manikin

**Questions it answers:** What does `0.5` mean, and who says so?

Every scalar dimension a species carries is a bare ratio in `[0, 1]`. A ratio
is a pure number: it carries no meaning at all until something declares where
its ends and its middle are. For most of this project's history that
declaration was a people. `0.5` meant *goblin* — because goblin was the first
people written down, and every downstream formula was then deliberately
constructed to reduce to its plain pre-species constant at goblin's row.
`0.5 + σ` is `1.0` exactly when `σ` reads goblin. The number was a datum, and
its frame was an inhabitant of the world the number measured.

That is sound as long as the roster has one member. It stops being sound the
moment the roster grows, because the frame is then simultaneously a *reading*
— one row in the same table as everything it anchors. A people that is both
the yardstick and a thing measured by the yardstick cannot be re-authored:
giving goblin a temperament of its own would move the origin of nine axes at
once, in every world that has ever contained a goblin, without a single
formula changing. A convention had quietly become a fact about the world.

The manikin is that frame lifted back out of the roster. It is a reference
vector — three mind scalars, one society scalar beside two enumerations, two
perception scalars beside one more — that belongs to no creature. A tailor's
manikin is built to no customer's measurements precisely so that every
customer can be measured against it. This one is the same construction, and
the same deliberate nobody.

This chapter's nine dimensions are `hornvale-species`'s three vectors (mind,
society, perception). A fourth vector, articulation, carries the identical
construction one domain over — see [Language](./language.md) for its own
`ArticulationVector::MANIKIN` and the third case its `tonality` dimension
adds to the vocabulary below.

**Why it is not a species.** The manikin has no identifier and no registry
row, but the sharper reason is physical rather than bookkeeping: it has no
mass, no metabolic class, and no resource or climate niche, so it has no
carrying capacity, so there is no density at which it could be placed
anywhere. It is not a kind that has been declined placement; it is a thing
with nothing to place. This matters because the obvious cheap alternative —
adding a colourless species and pointing the formulas at it — buys exactly
the failure it was meant to avoid: a kind that exists in the registry but in
no world, an unplaceable row that every consumer must nonetheless handle.

**The class of error being retired.** A number read against the wrong
reference surface is not slightly wrong; it is confidently wrong, and it
looks like data the whole way down. This project has already paid for that
once in the terrain stack, where an elevation compared against the wrong
datum made a people's documented highland stronghold read as unoccupiable —
no formula in error, every value in range, the conclusion false. A
psychological scalar anchored on one people is the same shape of mistake
waiting on a second cause.

**The lineage.** Reference bodies that are nobody's are old and standard
practice: the CIE standard observer, whose colour-matching functions describe
no actual pair of eyes; the ICRP's "standard man", a dosimetric body no
person has; the anthropometric manikin itself. The sharper analogy is
metrological. Anchoring a unit on a chosen exemplar is the Kilogramme des
Archives — a specific cylinder, in a specific vault, that *was* the kilogram
and therefore could never be found to be wrong by a milligram. Its
replacement was not a better cylinder. It was an invariant: a definition with
no artefact in it. The manikin makes the same move on a much smaller stage —
a unit is not a frame, and a frame should not be something that eats and
dies.

**The honest asymmetry.** Six of the nine dimensions are scalars, and for
those `0.5` is a genuine **neutral midpoint**: it is the middle of `[0, 1]`,
and it means the reading leans neither way. The other three are enumerations
— an authority shape, what earns standing, a waking schedule — and these have
no middle to occupy. `Hierarchic`, `Rank`, and `Diurnal` are therefore
**designated defaults**: chosen because a reference vector must read
*something*, not because they are neutral, average, or typical. They are also,
at present, goblin's variants. That is the residue of the old frame, recorded
here rather than dressed up, because a designated default that quietly
acquires the authority of a midpoint is precisely the error this chapter
exists to end.

There is a third case, and it does not appear among these nine — it belongs
to articulation, the fourth vector, over in `hornvale-language`. Most of that
vector's dimensions are the first kind, scalars sitting at the neutral
midpoint. `tonality` is neither kind cleanly: it *is* a scalar, so unlike
`Hierarchic`/`Rank`/`Diurnal` it does have a middle to occupy — and the
manikin deliberately does not sit at it. It reads `0.0`, atonal, the value
every shipped people currently carries, because every shipped body plan
affords no pitch contrast. A designated default can therefore wear a
scalar's clothes exactly as it wears an enumeration's; the tell is not the
axis's shape but whether the value was *chosen* or *derived*. Recording that
is the point of a chapter whose subject is a convention that had quietly
acquired the authority of a fact.

**Goblin, afterwards.** Goblin's authored row still sits at exactly the
manikin's values on every dimension, of every vector, all four of them.
Nothing in the model requires that, and the coincidence is now pinned by a
characterization test per vector family rather than assumed by the
arithmetic — so the day goblin is finally characterised on its own merits, it
arrives as a visible change to those tests, not as a silent re-origining of
the world.
