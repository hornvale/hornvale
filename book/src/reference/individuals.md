# The Four Kinds of Individual

A world holds people in four different senses, and they are not variations on
one thing. They differ on three axes at once — who drives the individual into
existence, whether the world writes it down, and what span of time it occupies —
and confusing two of them produces the wrong question about both.

```
  kind             who drives it   committed?   where it lives           span
  ---------------  --------------  -----------  -----------------------  ----------------
  founder          the world       YES          the person domain        a dated life
  roster member    the world       no           the vessel window        the present
  possessed body   the reader      no           the vessel window        the present
  lot              the reader      no           the lot window           a whole life,
                                                                          usually dead
```

**A founder** is the only individual the world remembers. When a community is
founded at a notable moment, the person domain draws a founder, names them, and
commits them: they have an identity in the record, a birth, a lineage, and
descendants that later communities can be shown to descend from. Everything
downstream can cite them. A founder is world-state, which means a founder is
also a save-format obligation — the draw that produced one consumes a stream,
and the label it consumes is a permanent contract.

**A roster member** is a body derived on demand in the standing present. The
vessel window builds them from the committed community tree when a reader
arrives somewhere, so that a settlement of forty people has forty people in it;
nothing about them is written down, and the source that derives them says so in
as many words. They are deterministic in the world and the place, so two
readers standing in one settlement see the same crowd — but the crowd is a
reading of the record, not an addition to it.

**A possessed body** is the roster member a reader is currently wearing. It is
the same kind of thing as a roster member, and it is listed separately because
the axis that separates them is *autonomy*: the world decides who stands in a
settlement, and the reader decides which of them to be. A possessed body's acts
can move world-state where the walk commits what it does; the body itself is
still derived.

**A lot** is one whole life, drawn from everyone who ever lived across the
world's deep history and told from the committed record alone. Its key is the
reader's — an index on the same footing as a world seed — and every choice
inside it is pure hash arithmetic over the world seed, the index and a label,
so nothing is drawn from the world and nothing is committed back to it. A lot
spans a life rather than a moment, and because the great majority of a world's
person-years were lived before its present, a drawn life is usually one that
ended centuries ago. See [The Lot](../chronicle/the-lot.md) and
[Ten Lives of Seed 42](../gallery/generated/the-lot-seed-42.md).

## The distinction that matters

Three of the four are uncommitted, which invites the reading that only the
founder is special and the rest are one kind of derived thing. The sharper
split is by **span**. A roster member and a possessed body exist in the standing
present, with a body, a position and a state that a verb loop can change. A lot
has no body and no position; it is a trajectory through space and time across
two thousand years, and the questions you can ask of it — where were they born,
what did their community survive, how old were they when it ended — are
questions no present-tense body can answer. That is why they are different
windows rather than two modes of one, and it is also what makes the bridge
between them (draw a life, then stand in it on a day of its own span) a real
piece of work rather than a configuration flag.
