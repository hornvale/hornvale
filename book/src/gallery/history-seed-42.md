# The Contested Clearing of Seed 42

A site read back out of the ledger by the `history` verb: the stratigraphy
of every people that ever settled one cell, oldest layer deepest, and the
derived flesh — the structures they raised, the residue in the grass
today. Nothing here replays the deep-history bake; it is all a
*present-as-query* over committed occupation facts, with the flesh
(structures, residue) derived on demand and never committed.

This is a real clearing on the world of seed 42 — cell 757 — and
fifteen hobgoblin steadings have risen on it, one settling atop the ruins of
the last, from the year 375 down to the present. Every one of the
fourteen completed layers ended at the hands of other hobgoblins — but
mostly not as defeats. Eight of the fourteen were not evictions at all:
the occupants had taken better ground from a neighbour and carried the
settlement onto it, so the layer closes on a departure. Only six fell to
a rival band. This is a people with only itself to fight, and on this
rise it is more often the one doing the taking.

The cold is in this column, but never as an ending. Not one layer
here fell to ice. Every layer instead *arrived* fleeing it — all
fifteen, the deepest included, driven off one of five distinct
neighbouring clearings. No one ever broke this soil by choice: the
ground has been a refuge from its first layer to its last, and a
staging ground for the next advance as often as a last resort. Only
one of the fifteen failed to last a single year. Read bottom to top,
the column carries its own technological arc: bronze-working at the
base, iron above it, classical at the top. The fifteenth was founded
in the year 1925 and stands yet, 75 years on: some 55 souls, two huts
and a granary, and no ruin yet to read.
