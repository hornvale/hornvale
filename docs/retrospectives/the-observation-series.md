# The Observation Series — retrospective

**Close state:** Complete at the user-approved technical scope boundary. The
product is the [chronicle](../../book/src/chronicle/the-observation-series.md);
the decision history is the [campaign ledger](../superpowers/ledgers/2026-09-09-hornvale-observation-series.md).

## What shipped

The campaign made a deterministic observation path from a manifest to a local
package. The CLI validates strict episode records and exports ordered packets
from an authoritative declared world; the Atlas preview consumes supplied
packet fields at phone and laptop viewports; the assembly checks contiguous
inputs and writes checksums without publishing. The capability audit then found
one witnessed public-scale path, the stellar neighborhood, and HV-009 exported
two byte-identical 900-packet sequences.

Firefox rasterized the 900 HV-009 frames at `1440×900`; ffmpeg assembled their
30-second MP4; `ffprobe` reported 900 frames at 30 fps; and the 1,802-entry
sidecar re-checked. Those are technical package facts. HV-009's manifest and
caption remain draft, with `approval: null`; no editorial state moved.

The reviews materially improved the boundary rather than merely accepting an
initial path: strict manifest/approval parsing, refusal for unobservable or
stale output, declared-world and seed provenance, repository-root resolution,
safe browser packet parsing, producer-owned count units, bounded frame indexes,
and checked package sidecars all landed in their respective fix rounds.

## The decisive finding

The renderer proves a technical pipeline, not the intended presentation. Its
shell loop invokes Firefox once for each packet, so HV-009 requires 900
browser launches. The result is a static Atlas text layout rasterized into a
film: it carries the correct supplied labels and evidence, but it is not the
fullscreen animated, subtitle-style, phone-legible presentation this series is
meant to become. Treating the completed MP4 as that presentation would confuse
an executable transport proof with an audience-facing renderer.
