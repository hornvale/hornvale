# 0115. A client's mirror may omit a channel, and the omission is the enforcement

**Status:** Accepted (2026-08-09, G6) · **Decider:** Nathan · **Relates:**
[0003](0003-trace-protocol-is-the-only-cross-domain-channel.md),
[0022](0022-sim-emits-data-clients-render.md),
[0114](0114-a-native-client-drives-across-the-linker-and-reads-across-the-serializer.md)

In the context of `vessel/session/v1`'s `social` channel, whose own doc comment
states that a pane rendering it unfiltered ships a cheat pane — a discipline the
schema documents and has no way to perform, since `social` is keyed by an entity
id that never appears in the knowledge store, so the join the mandate assumes
does not exist — we decided that **a client defines its own `Deserialize` mirror
of the wire schema, and that mirror may deliberately omit a channel**, with the
omission serving as the enforcement mechanism for a redaction the schema can
only describe.

## The decision

`hornvale-game-core` declares its own mirror types against `vessel/session/v1`,
exactly as `clients/vessel/src/snapshot.ts` already does in TypeScript, rather
than `windows/vessel` gaining a `Deserialize` derive. **The mirror does not
declare `social` at all.** serde ignores unknown fields, so the channel never
enters the client's address space.

The cheat pane is therefore not forbidden. It is unbuildable: there is no field
to render, no type to name it, and no error to suppress. The redaction is a
property of what was written down, not of what anyone remembered to check.

## Why an omission rather than a filter or a lint

A filter is code that can be wrong, bypassed, or refactored away by someone who
does not know why it is there. A lint is worth its next reader's attention. An
absent declaration is worth nothing to maintain and cannot regress: adding
`social` back is a visible, deliberate schema edit in the client, not a slip.

This is the same move as decision 0114's crate split one level down. There, a
forbidden *method* is unreachable because the crate that defines it is not a
dependency. Here, a forbidden *field* is unreachable because the type that would
hold it does not have it. In both cases the safety property is the absence of a
symbol.

It also inverts the usual objection to a hand-written mirror. A mirror is
normally a liability — a second copy that can lag. It is a liability here too
(see the consequence below), but it is *also* the only place where a consumer can
express "I decline to receive this", and a derive on the producer cannot express
that at all.

## What this record does not license

- **It is not permission to omit a channel the client needs and then guess at
  it.** The Quire's ways-on episode is the counter-example: the client omitted
  the room's exits from its mirror, and a reviewer then derived "no such datum
  exists on the wire" from *the mirror* rather than from the producer, which was
  false. An omission is a statement about what the client will not render, never
  evidence about what the producer emits. Read the producer.
- **It does not weaken the schema's doc.** The `social` mandate stays written
  where it is, because it binds every *other* consumer, including ones that have
  no mirror to omit from.

## Consequences

- **A provenance test can assert the omission rather than assume it.** Every
  non-blank cell of the composed grid traces to a named snapshot field; the
  source vocabulary has six variants and no `Social`, and the test is
  mutation-proven to discriminate. The redaction is checkable, not merely true.
- **The mirror lags silently unless something compares it.** A committed
  `vessel/session/v1` fixture, regenerated by `regenerate-artifacts.sh` and
  drift-checked, is what turns an upstream addition into a diff rather than an
  absence. Two are committed under `clients/game/core/tests/fixtures/` (one
  walk-band, one chamber-band).
- **A variant must name a datum that exists.** The Quire shipped and then deleted
  a provenance variant for the ways-on datum, on the reasoning that a category
  naming something the wire does not carry is itself a false provenance claim —
  precisely the failure the provenance test exists to prevent.
