## Entries

**#1 [Q] — Scope: one campaign or two?**

*Question.* The request named two deliverables: a technology corpus (an
instrument) and a replacement for `tech_for` (a world model, and an epoch).
Are they one campaign or two, and in which order?

*Decision.* **Two campaigns, corpus first.** The Kiln freezes the corpus and
scores today's world with it. A successor campaign replaces the model.

*Why.* Decision 0016 (measurement is preregistered) and decision 0936, which
made its freeze **structural** rather than promised by authoring its corpus
in a task that ran before any evaluation code existed. The same move is
available here, and F2–F4 guarantee the first score is a genuine
preregistered miss rather than a retrofit. Decision 0095 rejects by name a
corpus "drawn from what the world already models" as unable to falsify
anything — which is what sequencing the model first would produce.

*Alternatives discarded.* (b) One campaign, corpus and model together —
faster, one epoch, but the freeze degrades to a promise and nothing prevents
the corpus being shaped to what the new model happens to do. (c) Model
first, corpus after — cheapest route to a better world, forfeits
preregistration entirely.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* **None run.** Nathan selected option A
directly in conversation rather than this being auto-resolved under
autopilot; recorded explicitly per the backfill rule rather than left blank.

*Capture actions.* Successor campaign (the model replacement) to be captured
as an idea-registry row before The Kiln closes — see Follow-ups.


**#2 [Q] — Does 0136 clause 2 license an item whose components ask different
questions?**

*Question.* The first draft of the design decomposed a technology item into
four parts (prerequisites / acquisition / consequence / loss), each resolving
against a different basis, and justified it as "0136 clause 2 generalized."
Does the precedent actually reach that far?

*Decision.* **No — and the draft is withdrawn.** Clause 2 governs **two halves
asking the SAME question in two PLACES** ("is this capability implemented?" in
the sim, then in the client). It never contemplates components that ask
different questions, and claiming otherwise papered over the seam a reviewer
should press.

*Two findings from checking it.* (a) Clause 2's ordering is **never computed**:
`cli/src/systems.rs`'s `Verdict` enum has no combining logic, no `min`, no
"weakest" function — "takes its weakest half" is an **authoring discipline**,
and the resolver only re-checks that the authored verdict's anchor resolves.
(b) Consequently the ordering problem I raised (that `inapplicable` is
orthogonal to the `present`…`absent` scale, so `min` is undefined) is not a
latent bug in 0136 — the precedent simply never needs an order. `regularities/`
is the one family that **computes** a verdict and compares it against an
authored one (0936's two-way guard).

*Why it matters.* Had this shipped, the corpus would have needed a novel
total order over 0136's five verdicts, invented by this campaign, to combine
heterogeneous components. That is new machinery disguised as precedent.

*Alternatives discarded.* Defining a project-wide verdict order so `min`
becomes meaningful — rejected: it would bind all five sibling families to an
ordering none of them needs, to serve one corpus's internal arithmetic.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* 1 pass (operators `combination` +
`substitution`; organon `atlas`; dimensions `hierarchicalness`, `cyclicity`,
`modularity`). **Overturned the single-basis framing** I walked in with, and
the same pass's modularity prompt produced the four-part decomposition that
entry #4 then replaces.

*Capture actions.* Superseded by #4. Retained here rather than deleted: the
overreach is the reason #4 is shaped as it is.

**#3 [Q] — Does the corpus DECLARE its demands or DERIVE them?**

*Question.* Decision 0386 forbids both in one file. Which side does a
technology corpus take?

*Decision.* **Derive, by transitive closure over a prerequisite lattice** —
`the-ladder`'s shape (`read_derived`), not `the-merchant`'s (`read_declared`).
Each item names the one demand it `introduces` and the items it
`presupposes`; the demand set is computed on read and never written to the
file.

*Why.* 0386's own argument, verbatim: "**A derived set cannot under-describe,
because no human restates it**" — and its evidence is that a hand-written
demand list under-described itself **three times in a twelve-entry corpus**
(`m02` omitted adjectival predication, `m10` scored covered while being
unspeakable, `m07` credited for a construction its witness never built). A
technology catalogue is a prerequisite graph by nature, so the derived shape
is not merely safer here, it is the structure. 0261 supplies the second half:
materializing the closure back into the JSON would state one fact twice and
require an agreement test whose cheapest repair is deletion.

*Alternatives discarded.* Declared demands per item — rejected on 0386's
measured under-description rate. A hybrid (declared demands plus prerequisite
edges) — rejected outright: 0386 says a third shape **reopens that record
rather than extending it**.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* Covered by #2's pass; the `hierarchicalness`
prompt is what surfaced the lattice (multiple parents, genuine
incomparability, diamonds) as against a tree or a flat list.

*Capture actions.* None outstanding.

**#4 [Q] — One verdict per item, or two?**

*Question.* How does an item carry both "does the world model this
technology" and "does a people actually acquire, hold and lose it"?

*Decision.* **Two verdicts on two scales, never combined.**

- **Reach** — 5-valued per 0136 (`present`/`refused`/`deferred`/`absent`/
  `inapplicable`), one per derived demand, each citing an anchor the resolver
  re-checks; the item's reach is authored as its weakest demand, which is
  clause 2's discipline applied to **homogeneous** components (every demand
  asks the one same question: is this demand met?). **Ratchets.**
- **Trajectory** — computed from measurement over the committed census and
  compared two-way against an authored expectation, per 0936. **Can decay.**

*Why.* It dissolves #2's problem instead of solving it: there is no
heterogeneous `min` because the two questions never share a scale. Each layer
then sits squarely on an existing family's machinery — reach on
`tropes`/`systems`, trajectory on `regularities` — and neither needs a new
ordering. It also makes both halves resist optimism, which is `repertory/`'s
founding objection: a reach verdict cites a checked anchor, and a trajectory
verdict is computed, so **neither is satisfiable by declaration.**

*The consequence that makes it worth the cost.* Every item's trajectory is
`unmeasured` on freeze, because `tech_for` is a monotone clock (F2) — and
0936 invented `unmeasured` for exactly this: "frozen but not yet scored …
what allows a corpus to be authored in a task that runs before any evaluation
code exists." So the first column's headline finding is preregistered and
sharp: *of N technologies, zero are measurable for loss, because the model
cannot express loss.*

*Alternatives discarded.* A single fused verdict (needs #2's invented order).
Reach only, deferring trajectory to the successor campaign — rejected: the
trajectory axis is the campaign's actual motivation, and authoring it as
`unmeasured` costs nothing while making the successor's target explicit.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* Covered by #2's pass; the `cyclicity` prompt
is what separated ratchet from decay from trajectory and made the two-scale
split visible.

*Capture actions.* None outstanding.

**#5 [Q] — Does The Kiln freeze one corpus or two?**

*Question.* An invention catalogue is a **progress narrative** — ordered by
date of first attestation, organised on the assumption that technology
accumulates. That is the same assumption `tech_for` encodes (F2), so a corpus
drawn from it is **structurally blind to loss**, the axis that motivated the
campaign. Ship one column now, or two?

*Decision.* **Two corpora, both frozen by The Kiln**, measured on different
schedules: an **invention** catalogue (scores reach today) and a **collapse**
catalogue of technologies known to have been held and then lost (scores
trajectory, entirely `unmeasured` until the successor campaign builds loss).
Nathan's call, taken in conversation.

*Why.* Decision 0095: the output is a **matrix over corpora**, a single corpus
is one column, and *the disagreement between columns is the finding*. And
decision 0016: a freeze is only structural if it precedes the code that would
move it — the successor campaign **is** that code, so a collapse corpus
authored afterward is exactly the corpus "drawn from what the world already
models" that 0095 rejects as unable to falsify anything.

*Alternatives discarded.* One corpus now, the second registered for the
successor — precedented (`tropes/` shipped with Polti alone and added
`tvtropes-2012` later) and cheaper, but it buys that cheapness by surrendering
the freeze on the only axis the successor exists to build.

*Accepted cost.* The Kiln ships a column that reads entirely `unmeasured`.
That looks like nothing and is the preregistration working; the spec must say
so where a reader meets the column, not only here.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* Covered by #2's pass; the `cyclicity` prompt is
what exposed that an invention catalogue can only express one direction of
time.

*Capture actions.* Both corpora need idea-registry rows — **no existing row
proposes a technology corpus** (checked: 45 rows mention "corpus", none for
technology/invention/craft), so these are genuinely new per `docs/CLAUDE.md`'s
registry-first rule.

**#6 [Q] — Two verdict fields per item, or one?**

*Question.* Entry #4 specified two verdicts per item on two scales (reach and
trajectory). Does the sibling that already spans both do it that way?

*Decision.* **One field, with an extended vocabulary — #4's two-field shape is
withdrawn.** Read from the data rather than the decision text:
`regularities/sugarscape-1996.regularity.json` carries a single `verdict`
whose observed values are `absent`(24) `deferred`(9) `inapplicable`(6)
`grown`(3) `refused`(2) `flat`(1) — 0136's five and 0936's additions **in one
field**, not two.

*Why it works, and why it is better than #4.* The vocabulary is an implicit
**pipeline**: the measured values are only reachable if reach already
succeeded, so "reach passed" is encoded by the verdict being a measured one at
all. One field therefore loses no information, adds no structure, and matches
the one sibling that already faces this exact problem. #4's two fields would
have been this campaign's own invention sitting beside a sibling that had
already solved it — the same error as #2, one level up.

*The vocabulary.* 0136's five, plus 0936's `grown` / `flat` / `unmeasured`,
plus **one new value, `lost`** — acquired and then given up. It is the value
the whole campaign exists to make expressible, and it needs its own
justification in the spec exactly as 0936 justified its two.

*Anchors.* A measured verdict takes a `doc:` anchor, as regularities already
does (`anchor: "doc:book/src/domesday/settlement.md"`), admissible only
against a path `docs/generated-paths.txt` gives a generator.

*Alternatives discarded.* Two fields (#4) — withdrawn on the data above.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* No separate pass; this is a precedent read that
**overturned** #4's structure. Recorded rather than silently superseding it,
because the reason #4 was wrong is the campaign's recurring failure mode.

**#7 [Q] — The corpus is authored by people who know the model. How is the
freeze not compromised?**

*Question.* Decision 0016 wants the hypothesis frozen before the code that
would move it. We already know `tech_for` is a four-rung clock (F2). Selecting
40-80 items from ~1,500 with that knowledge is not blind, and could be biased
toward items we expect to score a particular way.

*Decision.* **A stated selection rule, independent of Hornvale's state, plus a
`disclosure` field on any item that is not blind.**

*Why.* Precedent is exact: sugarscape's `sug-wealth-skew` carries
`"disclosure": "NOT A BLIND TEST, disclosed under decision 0016: this
statistic's distribution was measured during the brainstorm that motivated The
Seedbed, before any corpus existed. Every other item in this corpus was
authored before its statistic was looked at."` The mechanism for an
unavoidably non-blind item already exists and is a **field**, not prose.

*What the selection rule must satisfy.* It must be applicable by someone who
has never read `history_bake.rs` — e.g. drawn from the catalogue's own
structure (its named story arcs, its dependency chains) rather than from a
judgement about what Hornvale would score well on. The rule goes in the
`provenance` field so a reader meets it before any number, per 0095.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* No pass; this is a direct precedent read of an
existing field in a sibling corpus.

*Capture actions.* None outstanding.

**#8 [Q] — The required ideonomy pass on #6/#7, and what it found**

*Question.* Entries #6 and #7 were resolved by precedent reads and recorded
**zero** ideonomy passes. `campaign-autopilot` names that exact
rationalisation ("this Q is precedent-answered, so ideonomy adds nothing") as
the overlay's documented first-campaign failure, and requires the precedented
answer be the *input* to a pass. Pass run before assembling the G3 package.

*Tuple.* Operators `abstraction-lift` + `tree-finding`; organon `map`;
dimensions `scope`, `size`, `source`.

*Three findings, two of which change the spec.*

1. **`lost` had no scope, and an unscoped verdict is the 0136 clause-2 bug.**
   A technology can be given up by one community, by a people, or by the
   world — three different claims. An instrument that picks silently per item,
   with the choice tracking the nicer answer, is precisely what clause 2 was
   written to fix, and it survived five reviews there because every anchor
   resolved. **Spec amended** (§5.2): `lost` means a people that held the
   capability no longer holds it, stated in the direction the resolver
   enforces.

2. **A bare verdict is blind to the pathology that motivated the campaign.**
   `tree-finding`'s empty branches surfaced it: F4's defect is not that
   Hornvale fails to acquire technologies, it is that **every** surviving
   community acquires **all** of them. Under a boolean `grown`/`flat`, "every
   people has bronze" and "half the peoples have bronze" score identically —
   so the instrument could not detect the defect it exists to detect. The fix
   is already in the sibling: sugarscape items carry a `statistic` and a
   `criterion` (`median-in-band`), and the verdict is whether the criterion is
   met. **Spec amended** (new §5.2a): a technology item carries a statistic
   over the distribution **across peoples**. Under such a criterion today's
   world scores `flat` (holding fraction 1.0, outside any band expressing
   divergence); a bare boolean would have reported `grown`.

3. **The two corpora carry incompatible theories of what a technology is.**
   `source` prompt: Asimov frames a technology as an individual invention
   (who, when); Henrich frames it as a distribution of skill in a population
   that can drift below a self-sustaining threshold. Under 0095 the
   disagreement is the finding, but it must be **declared**. Worth recording
   that the population framing is far closer to the project's own doctrine —
   TECH-1's "capability threshold the world crosses when a computed bar is
   cleared" is Henrich's shape, not Asimov's. **Spec amended** (§4.2).

*`abstraction-lift` (no spec change, carried for the successor).* Lifted, this
is "an instrument measuring whether a population sustains a heritable trait
against loss" — the shape of language death, island trait loss, and herd
immunity. Hornvale already ships `domains/demography` and
`domains/epidemiology`, which are threshold-and-population machinery the
successor campaign may be able to reuse rather than invent.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* 1 pass; **two spec amendments**, neither of
which any amount of re-reading would have produced — the divergence-blindness
in particular was invisible to a consistency review because the vocabulary was
internally coherent and precedented.

*Capture actions.* The `abstraction-lift` observation is a successor-campaign
note; carried in Follow-ups.

**#10 [Q] — How far past Henrich may the collapse corpus range?**

*Question.* Nathan accepted Henrich (2004) at G3 and added that we "can also
go more speculative." Every sibling corpus is a **single named source with a
year**, which is what lets one `provenance` field state one bias. What shape
does a wider corpus take without becoming the Hornvale-native corpus 0095

sibling convention rather than letting it read as one.

**#12 [G5] — What does `disclosure` mean in this family? (Task 1 review, M2)**

*Question.* Task 1 authored 41 items, all non-blind at corpus level (the
session had read the model), and gave `disclosure` to only 2 — reasoning that
`absent` is the default that cites nothing, so model knowledge cannot have
manufactured it. The reviewer objected that this inverts the sibling's
semantics: in `sugarscape-1996` the field marks per-item non-blindness, so a
reader applying that reading concludes the 39 bare items **were** blind, which
is the opposite of the truth. Left unruled it becomes family law, because
`technologies/CLAUDE.md` already generalises it.

*Ruling.* **`disclosure` marks per-item non-blindness, matching the sibling.
The "`absent` needs no disclosure" rule is REJECTED and inverted: an `absent`
whose verdict turned on having read the model owes a disclosure.**
Corpus-level non-blindness stays in `provenance`, where it already is, because
that is a fact about the whole instrument and not about any item.

*Why the implementer's rationale fails, specifically.* It assumed `absent` is
the conservative direction and therefore unfalsifiable by self-interest. For
**this** campaign the opposite holds: the thesis is that Hornvale's technology
model is impoverished, so a **high `absent` count is the flattering result**.
An item that is really `deferred` but scored `absent` is exactly the
self-serving error — and finding I1 is that error, instantiated: `BIO-8` names
a plough, `inv-turnplow` is a root scored `absent`, and the row is cited
nowhere in the file. A rule that exempts the flattering direction from
disclosure exempts the only direction that needed it.

*Binding consequence.* Task 4's resolver enforces this reading, and
`technologies/CLAUDE.md` must state it rather than the rejected one.

*Alternatives discarded.* Keep the implementer's rule and document the
divergence — rejected: two sibling families would use one field name for
opposite meanings, and the divergence would have to be re-learned by every
future reader. Require `disclosure` on all 41 — rejected as noise that would
make the field unreadable; it is corpus-level and belongs in `provenance`.

*Ideonomy passes / overturns.* None run; this is an adjudication of a review
finding against an existing sibling's observable semantics.

*Capture actions.* Folded into Task 1 fix round 1. Task 4 inherits it.

**#14 [G5] — A pattern in two rounds: the check's population was narrower
than the claim, both times on the flattering side**

*Not a ruling — a finding, recorded now because Task 10's retrospective needs
the mechanism and not just the instances.* Raised by Task 1's implementer
about its own work, unprompted, and confirmed against both rounds.

**Instance 1 (round 1 → found in round 2).** The disclosure check keyed on
**roots**; the rule it was said to enforce was **chosen vs inherited**. Roots
are a strict subset, so the check under-covered by exactly the items whose
prerequisites are all non-`absent`. `inv-parchment` was that item, and it sat
inside the commit that ratified the rule.

**Instance 2 (round 1 → corrected in round 2).** The weakest-demand rank
assertion was described as proving "the re-score licensed no downstream item
to rise." It constrains only items rising **above** a prerequisite. The
under-scoring direction satisfies `rank ≤` trivially, so the claim covered a
population the check never touched.

**The mechanism, which is the part worth keeping.** Both gaps fell on the same
side: the direction in which authored data can be too **LOW**. For this
campaign that is the *flattering* direction — the thesis is that Hornvale's
technology model is impoverished, so a high `absent` count is the result the
author wants, and a check blind to under-scoring cannot embarrass its author.
Neither gap was deliberate; both were invisible because the check's own output
was green and its stated purpose sounded like the rule.

**Round 2 then found the proxy was wrong in BOTH directions** — under-covering
by 1 and **over-covering by 6** (items with an `absent` prerequisite, whose
verdicts could not have moved). Over-coverage is the quieter half: a
disclosure on a forced verdict is noise that makes the real ones harder to
find, and it inflates the appearance of rigour. A proxy is not a conservative
approximation of a rule; it is a different rule.

**Instance 3, and it is the controller's** (round 2 → found in round 3). Ledger
#13's "is **now** `deferred`" implied the re-score created the counterexample.
The chosen set had been the same 12 items since the freeze; the re-score created
zero. My sentence described a population (*items the re-score made chosen*)
narrower than the claim it was offered as evidence for (*items the proxy
misses*). Corrected in place at `3eafbe435`.

**The implementer's own diagnosis is sharper than this entry's first draft and
replaces it.** Its words: *"the common factor is not that I check the wrong
thing — it is that I stop checking once the conclusion looks correct."* All
three instances fit: roots-vs-chosen (the conclusion "this item owes a
disclosure" was right), rising-vs-under-scoring (the conclusion "no item rose"
was right), re-score-vs-always (the conclusion "the proxy is wrong" was right).
In every case a true conclusion retired the audit of the reasoning that reached
it. That is a stopping rule, not a checking skill, and it is why three rounds
of competent review each found one more.

**The generalisation for this project.** When a check is written alongside the
thing it checks, state the **population** the check ranges over and the
population the **claim** ranges over, separately, and compare them as sets. A
check whose population is described only by its purpose ("enforces the
disclosure rule") cannot be audited against the claim, and the gap will fall
on whichever side the author is not motivated to look at. And the trigger for
doing that comparison cannot be suspicion — a correct conclusion is precisely
when it feels unnecessary.

*Destination.* `docs/retrospectives/the-kiln.md` (Task 10), as the campaign's
lead process lesson.
