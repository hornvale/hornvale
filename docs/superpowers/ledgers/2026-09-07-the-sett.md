## Reproductions (measured before any spec was written)

All figures below come from two throwaway probes,
`clients/game/bin/examples/sett_raster_probe.rs` and
`sett_cost_probe.rs`, run at `origin/main` `fff169c65`. Both are pure
geometry except the cost probe's timing section — no genesis, no world, no
terrain — so the whole sweep runs in seconds.

**Why they were written at all: The Newel's own probes were never
committed and no longer exist.** `newel_h1.rs`, `newel_grid.rs` and
`newel_rasters.rs` appear in no commit (`git log --diff-filter=A` over all
three names returns nothing), so R13 and R14 — the measurements decision #8
rests on — had no reproducible instrument. Every number this campaign
inherits had to be re-derived from scratch. Task 6 of the plan exists so
that this campaign does not repeat it.

### S3 — R14's window size is misattributed, and the percentages move with it

R14 says: *"Window taken as 53 x 27 boxes, the plate's size at an 80x24
terminal."* **The plate at an 80x24 terminal is 40 x 20.**
`spread::world_plate_width(80, 24)` returns 40 (all three of its bounds
coincide there) and `spread::content_height(24)` returns 20. 53 x 27 is the
plate at a **93x31** terminal.

Nothing geometric depends on it — every conclusion below was taken at both
sizes and agrees — but the reachability percentages scale with the window,
so at the enforced 80x24 floor they are **0.7312%** (seam) and **0.00119%**
(corner), not 0.950% and 0.00201%.

### S4 — R14's decisive prediction P1 was never run as written, and as
### written it separates the options

R14 froze this measure:

> **agreement(R)** = fraction of `(facet, word)` pairs with a neighbour
> where `box_R(neighbour) == ideal(word)`.

and this prediction:

> **P1.** Options 1 and 2 reach agreement = 1.000 and self-collision =
> 0.000 on every facet whose window is clean. This is by construction, so
> it is a CORRECTNESS CHECK on the probe, not a finding.

P1 was reported **held**, on this evidence: *"both build a duplicate-free,
hole-free window on a face interior, which is what '100% by construction'
cashes out as."* That is a different property. Duplicate-free-and-hole-free
is a statement about the *raster*; `agreement` as defined is a statement
about the raster **and the word**, and the word comes from `heading_rose`,
which assigns by BEARING.

Run as written, option 2's agreement is:

```
                      equatorial faces      polar caps
words in their ideal box   18432/18432  100.0%   3968/9216   43.1%
all eight correct at        2304/2304   100.0%    496/1152   43.1%
```

"By construction" holds only where the lattice frame and the compass rose
are the same frame, which is the four equatorial faces and nowhere else.

**This is the same defect shape this campaign is already writing up about
The Pavement's H1** (design section 5): a preregistered check cashed out on
an axis that could not fail. Recorded as decision 0907 rather than left in
a ledger, because it is now the second instance and the pattern is the
finding.

### S5 — a third raster exists, it was never considered, and it dominates

**The rose-chain raster.** Box `(j, k)` is the facet reached by `k` steps
along the observer's own N/S `heading_rose` chain and then `j` along that
facet's E/W chain. The picture is the movement rule iterated, so "the box
to the left" is "what the left key does" by construction rather than by
geometry.

It is invisible on the four equatorial faces — measured, the lattice frame
and the compass rose agree there at **18432/18432 words, 2304/2304 facets,
100.0%** — which is exactly why R14 did not find it: every equatorial
measurement it took returns the identical number for both.

Measured over 288 facets at a 41 x 21 plate, both step axes:

```
                                          opt 2 (lattice)   opt 3 (rose chain)
arrow keys land in their box, equatorial       100.0%             100.0%
arrow keys land in their box, POLAR CAPS        66.7%             100.0%
typed diagonals, polar caps                     66.7%              66.7%
picture unchanged under one step, equatorial   100.0%             100.0%
picture unchanged under one step, POLAR CAPS    66.7%             100.0%
up-error, polar caps (mean / max, degrees)   28.5 / 58.9         8.8 / 24.4
duplicate or blank boxes, 41 x 21               0 / 0              0 / 0
```

And the picture's own internal coherence — for every box, is the box drawn
to its right the one the right arrow reaches from *there*:

```
                          row coherence   column coherence
rose chain / equatorial        100.00%          100.00%
lattice    / equatorial        100.00%          100.00%
rose chain / POLAR CAPS         95.95%           76.55%
lattice    / POLAR CAPS          1.49%            1.40%
```

On the polar caps the lattice raster is a picture no key follows: from a
box that is not the observer's, the right arrow reaches the box drawn to
its right **1.49% of the time**. That is the reported bug, relocated from
the observer's own box to every other box on the screen.

**Option 3 needs no sim change, no new verb, no change to `heading_rose`,
and supersedes no decision.** It also satisfies decision 0117 more
directly than option 2 does: it *consults* the sim's own movement decision
(`hornvale_locale::heading_rose`) rather than the client re-deriving a
second frame of its own.

### S6 — the corner is where option 3 pays, and it is 0.0012% of a face

At and around a cube corner, rose-chain, 41 x 21 = 861 boxes:

```
              observer   blanks   repeats   distinct
  the corner facet itself     39      243        579
                1 step in     18      244        599
               4 steps in     15      160        686
              10 steps in      9        0        852
              20 steps in      0        0        861
```

Option 2's worst corner at the same window is 100 repeats and 0 blanks, so
**option 2 is better AT the corner and worse everywhere else.** The trade
is the same shape R14 itself used to prefer option 2 over option 1: a
failure 473x rarer is worth a failure that is worse when it happens. Here
the ratio is larger still — the corner region is 0.0012% of a face against
the polar caps' one third of the world.

### S7 — COST: the inherited argument is falsified, in both halves

The campaign's stated largest unknown. Addressing 861 boxes at the walk
rung — the only step any of these options changes; everything downstream
(corner weights, the elevation blend, reflectance, the glyph) is identical
under all three and deliberately not measured:

```
mercator (today)                0.2183 ms per redraw
lattice transport (opt 2)       0.9066 ms per redraw     4.2x DEARER
rose chain, no memo (opt 3)   114.1571 ms per redraw
rose chain, memoised (opt 3)   11.3099 ms cold, 1.0376 ms warm
   memo: 819 misses on the cold frame, 2127 misses over a 41-step walk
```

Both graph rasters cost MORE, not less.

**And the premise was wrong as well as the conclusion.** The design argues
the graph raster is *"very likely CHEAPER than the status quo … where the
Mercator raster resamples every box from lat/lon each frame."* It does not.
`clients/game/bin/src/tiles.rs`'s `TileCache` serves the walk band — it is
keyed `(pole_lat_bits, pole_lon_bits, depth, tile_row, tile_col, light)`
over 32-box-square tiles, `Driver::world_plate_for_redraw` passes
`&self.window` to it unconditionally, and its own tests exercise
`plate::BAND_B_RUNG` directly. A warm redraw resamples nothing at all.
Today's whole walk-band redraw, everything included, is **0.3801 ms per
keypress** at 104x56 (median of 5 runs, 40 keys each, load average 2.94).

So the honest statement is the opposite of the inherited one: the graph
raster costs about **1 ms more per keypress**, and the question is whether
that matters rather than whether it is free. On a terminal client whose
keypress already runs a sim turn, it is small in absolute terms — but it
is a regression and it is named as one, not absorbed.

**S8 makes it recoverable.**

### S8 — 100% stability is what licenses an incremental raster, and only
### option 3 has it

Because the rose-chain picture is exactly the old picture shifted after a
step (100.00% of the overlap unchanged, on both bands, on both step axes),
only the newly exposed edge — 21 boxes for an E/W step, 41 for an N/S one —
needs building. That is the design's own *"a step shifts the window and
only ~27 boxes are new"* argument, which turns out to be **true of option
3 and false of option 2**: option 2's overlap on the polar caps is 66.7%
unchanged, so an incremental fill there would be wrong.

Not built in this campaign unless the measurement in Task 7 says it is
needed. Recorded so the next reader knows the headroom exists and what
licenses it.

## Decisions

### #1 [G1] — which raster, superseding The Newel's #8

**Question:** the inherited decision is option 2, the lattice-transport
raster. Does the measurement still support it?

**Decision:** **No. Option 3, the rose-chain raster.** The picture is the
compass rose iterated outward, so the key and the box agree by
construction at every facet where `heading_rose` is total.

**Why:** S5. Option 3 matches option 2 exactly on the four equatorial
faces (where they are the same raster) and beats it on every measured axis
on the polar caps — arrow agreement 100% against 66.7%, stability 100%
against 66.7%, internal coherence 96%/77% against 1.5%/1.4%, up-error 8.8
mean against 28.5. It changes nothing sim-side and supersedes no decision.
Option 2's advantage is confined to the eight cube corners, 0.0012% of a
face (S6), against polar caps that are a third of the world.

**Why the inherited decision was not wrong on the evidence it had:** R14's
own predictions were the right ones. P1, run as written, separates the
options; it was cashed out at readout as a weaker property and so never
did (S4). Every other R14 figure this campaign re-measured reproduces.

**Alternatives discarded:** option 2 (S5, S7 — dearer AND less correct);
option 1, lattice space (R14, unchanged — the seam hole); option 0, a
height-matched Mercator (R14 P4: it does not exist, `virtual_dims` already
derives that height); binding the arrow keys to the lattice frame instead
(measured: the worst compass word would point **58.931 degrees** off its
own bearing on the caps, 5.73% of words past 45 degrees, against
`heading_rose`'s measured and two-sided-pinned ceiling of 34.578 — a
fidelity cut, and it buys nothing option 3 does not give free).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass, 1 overturn, 3 enrichments.**
Tuple: abstraction-lift + cross-domain-reinstantiation; organon
`timeline`; prompts longevity / decomposability / predictability. The lift
("a chart of a curved surface must choose which of two connections to be
flat in, and the choice decides which promise it can keep") re-instantiated
into knitting-pattern drafting, where a chart is flat in stitch-and-row
count and never in geometry, and curvature is concentrated into named local
features (darts, short rows) rather than smeared. **The overturn is the
corner presentation** — the inherited design prefers a repeated box to a
blank one; the re-instantiation inverts it, because a blank at the one
facet where the surface actually folds is a local honest feature, and the
sim already has the sentence for it (`CORNER_BEARING_REFUSAL`: "The land
folds away to nothing that way"). The three enrichments are ledgered as
#2, #3 and #4 below.

**Capture:** design amendment §A1-A3; decision 0906; registry row
`NAV-two-frames-lattice-north-and-astronomical-north` updated from `raw`.

### #2 [G2] — the inverse map is the deliverable, not the raster

**Question:** what is actually being built?

**Decision:** the raster AND its inverse, `FacetId -> (col, row)`, built in
the same pass. Every overlay the walk band draws — point sites, rivers, the
perception layer, the observer's own mark — is placed today by
`mercator::project`, and each becomes a lookup in that map.

**Why (longevity prompt):** the Mercator raster has lasted five campaigns
and accreted four layers onto itself. Whatever replaces it is long-lived
infrastructure, and the thing every later feature will reach for is not
"which facet is in box (j,k)" but "which box holds this facet". The
inherited design names neither, and an overlay left projecting through
Mercator onto a graph raster would land in the wrong box silently.

**Alternatives discarded:** re-projecting overlays through Mercator (they
would disagree with the terrain under them); keeping a second Mercator
window alongside (two frames, which is the bug).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** enrichment 1 of #1's single pass; no
separate pass.

### #3 [G2] — the change decomposes into four independent parts

**Question:** is "swap the raster" atomic?

**Decision:** no — addressing, the inverse map, the centring, and the
cache are four separately shippable and separately testable changes, and
the plan is written on them. Only the first is what the inherited design
describes.

**Why (decomposability prompt):** the window's `origin_row`/`origin_col`
stop meaning anything at the walk rung (the observer is central by
construction, so `follow_the_walker`'s per-turn re-centring becomes
structural), and the chart-tile cache key cannot survive (a graph raster
has no chart-tile coordinates) and is replaced by a facet-keyed memo, whose
hit rate S8's stability result guarantees.

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** enrichment 2 of #1's pass.

### #4 [G2] — a corner is never diced

**Question:** what does a box the chain cannot reach draw?

**Decision:** nothing — a blank. Not a seeded draw among the candidates,
not a new seam glyph.

**Why (predictability prompt, and precedent):** The Newel's own R12 ruled
on the identical shape for The Stipple's biome boundary — a seeded draw is
"honest per value and dishonest per pattern", manufacturing structure the
underlying field does not have. A diced corner would manufacture terrain.
A new glyph for 0.0012% of the world does not earn a place in decision
0389's ladder. The blank is the local, honest mark at the one place the
surface folds, and it is reachable only within ~10 steps of one of eight
points.

**Alternatives discarded:** a seeded draw (R12); a dedicated seam glyph
(0389's budget, and 0.0012% reachability); repeating the last reachable
facet (that is option 2's failure mode, chosen against in #1).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** enrichment 3 of #1's pass — and the
OVERTURN recorded there, since the inherited design states the opposite
preference.

## Follow-ups

- **Decision 0510's vi-key diagonals are not implemented.** 0510 rules that
  "diagonals are bound as a convenience (the vi-keys `y`/`u`/`b`/`n`)".
  `clients/game/bin/src/input.rs`'s `Focus::Walk` arm has no such binding;
  its catch-all is `KeyCode::Char(c) => Action::FocusAndType(c)`, so those
  four keys focus the CLI and type themselves. Not this campaign's subject
  (the report is about the arrow keys) and not silently absorbed either.
  Matters here because the residual this campaign accepts — typed diagonals
  landing in the diagonal box 66.7% of the time on the polar caps — sits on
  the surface 0510 already calls secondary.
- **`chart.rs`'s projection defect** (The Newel R8) is resolved by deletion
  rather than repair, under this campaign's Task 5. Recorded here because
  the inherited design lists "revive it or delete it" as an open question
  and this is the answer.
