# The Weft — decision ledger

The campaign that builds the **derived surface tier**: a per-facet feature
surface computed from noise interacting with macro state, closing the density
gap The Prospect measured. Decision block **0686-0695**.

The name: the **warp** is the macro fields already running continuously through
the world (`blend_at` over per-vertex fields); the **weft** is the fine detail
that crosses them at facet resolution. The weaving metaphor is not decoration —
it is where the campaign's correlation-length parameter came from (entry #2).

---

### #1 [Q, Nathan] — what "something interesting" means at 1.126 km

**Question.** The aim "every square mile, something interesting" is satisfiable
very differently depending on whether the dense thing is a place you *enter* or
something you merely *notice*. No precedent: this is world-design intent, not
derivable from the decision log, the registry or the code. Candidate answers
diverge materially (derived interiors vs. derived prose), so it was surfaced
rather than auto-resolved.

**Decision (Nathan, verbatim intent).** Graded — texture common, enterable
places rarer — with four riders:

1. **The aim is 1 per FACET, not 1 per square mile.** A facet is 1.126 km on a
   side and a square mile is 2.04 of them, so this doubles the target.
2. **Enterable places keep being added** "until I've run out of ideas". It is
   entirely possible most facets, or *all* facets, end up carrying an enterable
   feature.
3. **Do not rule that out constitutionally.** No mechanism may structurally cap
   enterable density.
4. **Per-kind frequency must be tunable, relatively and absolutely,**
   "individually and severally", as kinds keep being added.

**Consequence the campaign must carry.** The published gap figure moves:
84,200x, not the 41,200x now in `book/src/open-questions.md`, which was written
against the square-mile framing on 2026-09-03. **Re-scoring that Gradient entry
is part of this campaign's Definition of Done** (decision 0030).

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* 0 / 0 — a direct question to Nathan whose answer
is his intent, not a proposition to expand. Riders 3 and 4 became hard
constraints on entry #2, where a pass was run.

*Capture:* this entry; the DoD note above; constraint carried into #2.

### #2 [G1] — the mechanism is per-kind prevalence fields

**Question.** How does a facet acquire a derived feature? Three candidates:
(A) per-kind continuous prevalence fields sampled per facet; (B) one
significance scalar whose range is partitioned into kind bands; (C) multi-scale
— a mid-scale locality-character field conditioning per-facet detail.

**Decision. (A)**, with the modulating field as `Fbm` and discrete occurrence
drawn per facet from the prevalence.

**Why.** An ideonomy pass (cross-domain re-instantiation + combination, over a
lattice organon, dimension-prompts predictability/side-effect/autonomy)
collapsed the three candidates into one by ordering them:

- **(B) is (A) plus a constraint, and the constraint is the thing #1 forbade.**
  Partitioning one scalar's range into kind-bands forces the per-kind
  prevalences to sum to 1 — a simplex constraint — so raising enterable density
  necessarily lowers something else and "individually and severally" becomes
  unsatisfiable *by construction*. B is the obvious design and it is
  disqualified structurally, not by taste.
- **(C) is already inside (A) for free.** A mid-scale locality character is a
  multi-octave modulating field, and `Fbm::new(seed, octaves)`
  (`kernel/src/noise.rs`) ships today. There is no second mechanism to build.

*Precedent cited.* `cave_process` -> `presence_prob`
(`domains/terrain/src/features.rs`) is already exactly (A) at **vertex**
resolution: proneness from macro state, modulated, then a presence probability.
The campaign moves an established pattern down two orders of magnitude rather
than inventing one. `blend_at` (`windows/locale/src/lib.rs`) supplies macro
state continuously at facet resolution, and The Hachure (Stage 1) has just
shipped the same continuous-below-the-grid move for height.

*Alternatives discarded.* (B) — see above, structural. (C) — not discarded but
**absorbed**: it is (A) with octaves.

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* **1 / 0.** No overturn; substantial enrichment.
Three design elements appeared that none of the three candidates had:

1. **Correlation length is a first-class per-kind parameter, expressed in
   facets-of-travel** (from weaving: a motif repeating below the eye's span
   reads as texture, at it as rhythm, above it as pattern). It is separate from
   frequency and decides whether a kind reads as ground cover or as landmark.
2. **The legibility metric can be a standard spatial-autocorrelation statistic**
   (Moran's I / Ripley's K) rather than a bespoke one — from epidemiological
   prevalence mapping, which already separates a continuous prevalence surface
   from discrete incidence draws exactly as (A) does.
3. **"Feels found" has an exact, measurable meaning.** Registry row
   `KNOW-uncertainty-is-epistemic` already settles that in a deterministic world
   every doubt is incomplete observation, never a dice roll. So a place feels
   *found* iff **a knowledgeable observer could have predicted it from visible
   signs and a naive one could not** — predictability-from-antecedents, not
   variety.

*Capture:* this entry. Elements 1-3 go into the spec's design section.

### #4 [G2] — a residency window over the function, and features by composition

**Question.** Where does the derived surface's cost actually go, and what holds
a materialized feature between derivation and use?

**Decision.** Three layers, and the middle one is new to the design:

| layer | what it is | lifetime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the function | per-kind prevalence x occurrence, pure in `(seed, level, facet, kind)` | none — recomputed at will |
| the residency window | materialized feature COMPONENTS for facets near the observer | bounded; evicted when it stops earning its keep |
| the ledger | untouched | — |

A feature kind is a **bundle of components**, never a subclass. Discovery is
observation-scoped (proximity, visit, or rumor), so evaluation is bounded by
what is observable rather than by the world.

**Why (precedent cited).**

- **Composition is the constitution one level down.** CLAUDE.md: *"Adding a
  domain must never require editing an existing one."* A component bundle makes
  adding feature kind N+1 an append that touches nothing existing — which is
  exactly what #1's rider 4 demands (keep adding kinds indefinitely, tune each
  individually and severally). Inheritance would make every new kind a
  negotiation with the existing taxonomy.
- **Lazy, observation-scoped evaluation is not an optimisation.**
  `KNOW-uncertainty-is-epistemic` establishes that the world is a total function
  and an observer is a partial evaluation of it. Evaluating only what is
  observed *is* what observation means here; the performance win and the
  epistemics are the same fact.
- **The store already exists and eviction is already proven safe.**
  `kernel/src/derived.rs`'s `Derived<K, V>` with `Validity::Pure` — and the doc
  states our exact case: *"World-derived is not a third class — it is `Pure`
  with the world's identity … folded into the key."* It carries `evict(key)` and
  `evict_all()`, and a **chaos-eviction property battery** already evicts at
  every legal opportunity to prove correctness is unaffected. So a window under
  any eviction policy is safe by construction, with the battery pinning it.
- **The prohibition that still binds.** The Terrier: *"do NOT memoize inside
  this function, because a hidden cache in a derivation path is how derived
  state stops being derived."* Its prescription (hoist to the caller) is what
  shipped and the prohibition stands. Caller-owned, `&mut`-threaded, no
  `RefCell`/global/`OnceLock`, and a test pinning cache-present byte-identical
  to cache-absent — the contract `blend_at_cached` already states.

**The cost model this buys, to be preregistered as a budget.** Per-tick tax is
**zero** when the observer does not move (the window is unchanged, nothing
re-derives). Movement tax is **perimeter, not area**: stepping one facet evicts
a trailing edge and fills a leading one, ~84 facets per step at radius 10
against ~441 held. That ratio is what decides whether the radius is 10 or 40, so
it is a measurement, not a guess.

**Rumors are degraded observations, never fabrications (Nathan).** In-game
rumors must not become wild goose chases. The epistemic frame supplies the
generator: a rumor descends from an actual observation, worn by transmission, so
its error modes are **stale**, **vague** (precision decays per hop, yielding a
region rather than a point) and **misattributed** — never "rolled false". The
existing seam is already correct for this: `windows/vessel/src/knowledge.rs`
splits projection-derived entries (re-derivable, enforced by
`knowledge_is_subset`) from **heard** entries, which are *deliberately* outside
that contract because "heard != true is the epistemic point" (The Echo). A
rumor is a heard entry and is already permitted to be wrong. `Knowledge` is
per-agent and serialized into session snapshots, **not** the ledger, so
discovery state has a sanctioned non-ledger home today.

**Invariant to preregister:** a rumor names a REGION, and the region is never
empty. Following one always yields something; the failure mode is "not what you
were promised", never "nothing at all". That is the anti-frustration guarantee
stated as a test rather than left to tuning.

**Constraint banked for whenever a general component store is built.**
Component storage **must not be keyed on `TypeId`**. `derived.rs` gives the
reason it is one map per shape: *"`TypeId`'s ordering is not build-stable … a
`BTreeMap<TypeId, _>` would put an unstable iteration order under a
byte-identity guarantee."* Explicitly registered stable component ids instead.
Cheap now; discovered otherwise as a byte-identity failure reproducing on one
host and not another.

**Two errors of mine this entry corrects, recorded because a correction is
unaudited text.**

1. I claimed *"derived features cannot live in an ECS, because an ECS
   enumerates a materialized population and the point of derivation is that the
   population is never materialized."* Wrong: the point of derivation is to
   avoid paying **before the need exists**, not to forbid holding the result. A
   bounded window is consistent with derivation, and "cheaper to keep than to
   regenerate" is the right test. Nathan's correction.
2. I implied `Derived` was append-only and therefore unsuitable as a window. It
   has `evict`/`evict_all` and a battery proving eviction-safety. I read
   `RoomMeshMemo`'s append-only USAGE as a property of the type. Caught by
   grepping the type before asserting — which is the only reason it did not
   reach the spec.

*Alternatives discarded.* A hidden cache inside the derivation (forbidden by
The Terrier, and it is how derived state stops being derived). An unbounded
memo with no eviction (a walking session's key space is unbounded, so it leaks).

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* **0 / 0, stated explicitly rather than left
blank.** This is a G2 section, not a G1 or a nontrivial `Q`, so the
per-decision pass is not mandated. It also arrived through Nathan overturning my
framing directly, which did the adversarial work a pass would have done — and
the record shows it overturned two of my claims, not zero.

*Capture:* this entry; the `TypeId` constraint and the rumor invariant carry
into the spec; the general-ECS question stays undecided and out of scope here.

### #5 [Q, Nathan] — placed/derived is about WHEN a feature is generated, not what kind of thing it is

**My error, corrected by Nathan.** I proposed a criterion: *"Derived if
existence is explained by present conditions; Placed if existence implies a past
event."* That is a **semantic** axis and it is the wrong one. It reads plausibly,
it sorts the current three kinds correctly by accident, and it would have gone
into the spec as a rule for classifying every future kind.

**The actual axis.** Placed = generated at **worldgen time**, at the
41K-vertex resolution that is all worldgen can afford; present in the model, so
it can affect and be affected by the rest of the world. Derived = generated at
**observation time**, at walkable scale, spawned around the observer — and
therefore unable to participate in world-affecting events *until it is derived*.
Nathan's framing: **placed is Dwarf Fortress, derived is Caves of Qud.**

**Why caves exist at both levels — the question my criterion could not answer.**
Not because caves are a hybrid or a boundary case. Because you want
world-affecting events involving caves, and only a placed feature can carry one.
A placed cave is a Dwarven Kingdom that can be invaded and have its fall
recorded; a derived hollow is somewhere a traveller gets out of the rain. Same
kind of thing, two tiers, both legitimate, and the SAME kind may legitimately
exist at both.

**Consequence for the design.** Nothing forbids a derived twin of a placed kind,
so my objection to overhangs — that they would duplicate caves and reproduce the
"two disagreeing answers to *is there a cave here*" bug `site.rs` warns about —
was wrong on its own terms too. That warning is about two **predicates** for one
placed cave, not about two tiers. **Overhangs stay** (Nathan): a place that is
not enterable but where a traveller can shelter from rain and start a fire is
utility and interest, and that is enough.

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* 0 / 1 — no pass was run; Nathan overturned it
directly. Recorded because the overturn is the content: a criterion of mine that
sorted today's data correctly and generalised wrongly is the exact failure mode
a pass exists to catch, and I did not run one before proposing it.

*Capture:* this entry; the corrected axis governs the spec's tier language.

### #6 [survey] — what a walker currently perceives at a settlement, a ruin, and a chamber

Run at Nathan's request against committed output (`book/src/gallery/`) and
source, rather than reasoned about. Three findings.

**1. A WALKER NEVER PERCEIVES A RUIN. Not thinly — not at all.** `brief.rs`
states it as a deliberate choice: the brief *"does NOT carry the fields no
consumer reads yet. The ruin signature (`cause`, `ended_by`, ages) and the
district vocabulary are absent on purpose."* Ruins exist as ledger facts
(`is-ruin`, emitted from dead occupations, queryable by `ruins_of_people`); the
almanac reads them; the lab measures `first-day-is-ruin`; the walk band is
silent. You can stand on a dead civilisation and be told "tropical seasonal
forest, on a rise". This is a **placed** feature with no walker-facing surface,
so it needs a READER, not a generator.

**2. The placed tier is more monotonous than assumed, and monotony is therefore
not purely a resolution problem.** Seed 42's 103 placed exotic sites carry five
distinct descriptions:

```
68  under a fungal canopy                                     66%
16  grown with mineral crystal, with biota found nowhere else
 8  warmed from below
 8  under a fungal canopy, with biota found nowhere else
 3  fed by cold seeps, crusted with microbial mats
```

74% mention a fungal canopy. Variety was cheap at 103 items and was not taken,
so "derive it at facet scale" is not automatically the cure for sameness.

**3. Chambers are rudimentary in CONTENT, sound in MECHANISM.** Real output:
*"A small room in Doaba, holding a doorway and a screen."* -> *"…a doorway and
an alcove."* Meanwhile `chamber.rs` describes exactly the tier this campaign is
building: *"existence and content are pure functions of its address, and nothing
about a chamber is ever stored."* The derived tier already exists underground;
this campaign brings it to the surface.

*Incidental defects observed in the same transcript, recorded so they are not
lost:* all 64 settlement residents are listed as "Here:" inside every chamber;
and `examine` emits *"You see no a fern-choked draw…"* (article agreement). Both
outside this campaign's scope.

### #7 [Q, Nathan] — the campaign takes BOTH halves

**Question.** #6 splits the target in two. Only the unbuilt kinds (springs,
thickets, overhangs, erratics) need a derived surface; ruins need a *reader* for
information that already exists. Does The Weft build the derived surface only,
or also give the placed tier its missing walker-facing surface?

**Decision (Nathan).** **Both.**

**Why.** They share the delivery machinery — each ends as something the brief
reports and the prose renders — and the placed half is the cheaper, larger
immediate win: the information is already generated and merely unread. It is
also the stronger sign-legibility source, because a ruin is evidence about a
real past event rather than about present conditions, which is the one thing a
derived feature structurally cannot be (#5).

**Cost acknowledged.** This is a two-half campaign and larger than the density
brief alone. That matches the standing preference for heavyweight campaigns
(the per-campaign toll punishes small ones), and the halves are separable at
plan-stage boundaries if it needs to be cut.

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* 0 / 0 — a scope call put to Nathan with the
survey in hand; he took both. No pass run.

*Capture:* this entry; both halves carry into the spec.

### #8 [G2, MY ERROR] — the spec's own arithmetic was wrong, and the self-review caught it

**What happened.** The spec's first draft claimed `book/src/open-questions.md`'s
"5.8% of that ceiling" and "buys about 17x" **did not reproduce**, and
commissioned a Task 0 to reconcile a denominator mismatch. Both figures
reproduce exactly. The error was mine: I computed the placement ceiling as
`402,653,184 / 40,962 = 1 in 9,830`, which is a **one-kind** ceiling.

**The correct arity.** `SiteReason` has two variants, `Cave` and `Exotic`, and
`site_facet_for` places one facet per `(vertex, reason)`. So the ceiling is
**1 in 4,915**, and every published figure follows from it:

```
  4,915 / 84,200          = 5.8%     today's share of the ceiling
  84,200 / 4,915          = 17.1x    what saturating the placed tier buys
  4,915 * 0.489           = 2,403x   the ceiling gap vs a per-square-mile target
```

**What is actually stale in the book, and it is only one thing.** The TARGET.
`site_density.rs` reasons against ~0.489 sites per facet (one per square mile,
since a facet is 1.126 km and a square mile is 2.04 facets); Nathan's aim is one
per facet. So 41,200x -> 84,200x and ~2,400x -> ~4,915x, while 5.8% and 17.1x
are target-independent and must not be touched. `site_density.rs` had already
anticipated this — "the earlier 84,200x / 4,900x compared against a rate of 1" —
and set the figures aside only because the target was then per square mile.

**Why this one is worth recording rather than quietly fixing.** It is the exact
failure the autopilot's verification rule exists for, and it nearly shipped as a
*task*: a spec commissioning work to fix a defect that did not exist, in a
document that was correct, on the strength of arithmetic I did not check against
the code. The thing that caught it was reading `site_density.rs` to verify the
84,200 figure I was quoting — not re-reading my own paragraph, which had been
internally consistent and wrong. Re-reading cannot catch a wrong belief; it
checks a claim against the model that produced it.

*Alternatives discarded.* Leaving Task 0 in as harmless diligence: it would have
sent an implementer to "reconcile" two documents that already agree, and the
most likely outcome of that task is an edit making one of them wrong.

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* 0 / 0 — a factual correction, not a design fork.

*Capture:* this entry; spec §1.1 rewritten as a reconciliation rather than a
task; §10's DoD item narrowed to a target substitution.

### #9 [G4] — the plan, and two spec defects its self-review found

**Decision.** `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-03-the-weft.md`: ten tasks, Half A
first (Tasks 1-3, the ruin reader) because it delivers value without any new
mechanism, then Half B (Tasks 4-8), then the preregistered readout (Task 9) and
close (Task 10). Self-reviewed against the spec; both halves covered.

**Defect 1 — the spec named an enum variant that does not exist.** §4.3's
branch table said "emit `Extent::Region`". `Extent` has exactly one variant,
`Point`. Worse, `site.rs` already carries **four** doc corrections stating that
`Extent::Region` does not exist, each cleaning up a draft that asserted it did —
so this spec was the fifth instance of a documented, named trap. Caught by
reading the enum before writing the task, not by re-reading the spec. §4.3 now
states the absence, and Task 3 makes adding the variant a branch with its own
cost (an enum widening whose one real match is exhaustive, so the compiler
enumerates the work).

**Defect 2 — a preregistered hypothesis nothing could test.** §7 preregistered
**H4**, "every rumor names a region, and the region is never empty". The
campaign builds **no rumor producer**: §5.5 names rumors as an existing seam
(`knowledge.rs`'s heard entries), not a deliverable. A test of H4 would
therefore have passed by finding no rumors — a guard that is green because its
population is empty, which is the exact vacuous shape this repo has shipped five
times and which its own instruments (The Ford's anti-vacuity companion) exist to
prevent.

**Ruling.** H4 and the rumor producer are deferred **together**, and the spec is
amended NOW rather than at close, so the two documents agree for the campaign's
whole life. Building the producer was the alternative and is rejected: it is a
third half on a campaign already carrying two, and Nathan's scope call (#7) took
two.

**Why both defects are mine and both are the same kind.** Each was a claim about
something outside the document — an enum's variants, a subsystem's existence —
written confidently from reasoning rather than checked against the code. The
autopilot's verification rule names exactly this, and the plan is where it bites
hardest: a plan's claims become an implementer's instructions, and an
implementer who trusts them writes the defect for you.

*Alternatives discarded.* Leaving H4 in and letting Task 9 discover it: the
likely outcome is a green vacuous test, because a hypothesis with an empty
population does not announce itself.

*ideonomy passes / overturns:* 0 / 0 — plan authoring and its self-review, not a
design fork.

*Capture:* this entry; spec §4.3 and §7 amended; Task 3 and Task 10 carry the
two branches; a registry row for the rumor producer is owed at Task 10.

### #10 [Task 3] — `Extent` stays `Point`; a dead occupation's territory is not recoverable

recoverable from `OccupationRecord` alone, which would justify widening

**Answer: no, and the branch taken is `Extent` unchanged, `Point` emitted.**

presentation task is out of scope for this campaign.

**What was done instead.** `windows/vessel/src/site.rs`'s `Extent` doc gained
one paragraph stating the reason, beside the file's four existing
`Extent::Region`-does-not-exist corrections (not restating them). Registry row
`SURF-ruin-territory-needs-worldgen-extent` files the deferred case: an
occupation carrying a real extent at worldgen time, so a later campaign can
give a ruin ground rather than a point.

### #11 [Task 6] — the residency window: measured movement tax, and a kernel addition the brief didn't name

**What was built.** `windows/worldgen/src/weft/window.rs`: `WeftWindow`,
wrapping `Derived<WeftKey, Vec<WeftFeature>>` where
`WeftKey = (Seed, u32 /*globe level*/, FacetId, WeftKind)`, exactly as the
brief specified. `features_at` is the get-or-derive read (cache hit on the
store, else `prevalence` + `occurs`, held from then on). `advance_to(centre,
radius, geo, index, pack, seed)` computes the new Chebyshev disc (BFS over
`Facet::neighbors()`, `(2·radius+1)²` facets away from a cube seam),
diffs it against the previously-resident set, evicts the trailing-edge
facets' entries (all kinds) and fills the leading-edge facets' entries (all
kinds) — never touching a facet that stays resident. `WeftKind::ALL` (added
to `kinds.rs`) is the one place a new kind (Task 7) must be added for the
window to enumerate it.

**Decision not named in the brief: `Seed` needed `Ord`.** `Derived<K, V>`
requires `K: Ord`, and `Seed` (`kernel/src/seed.rs`) derived only
`PartialEq, Eq, Hash` — not `PartialOrd`/`Ord`. `WeftKey`'s literal shape
`(Seed, u32, FacetId, WeftKind)` cannot be a `BTreeMap` key without it. Added
`PartialOrd, Ord` to `Seed`'s derive list (delegating to the wrapped `u64`'s
natural order) rather than restructuring the key to avoid `Seed` in first
position — the brief's key shape is a save-format-adjacent contract stated
verbatim from Task 5's shipped signatures, and `FacetId` already derives
`Ord` for the identical reason, so this brings `Seed` in line with the
project's other identity newtypes rather than introducing a new pattern.
This is a kernel-layer edit (costs more in `gate-commit`'s compilation
units, per the root `CLAUDE.md`'s gate-ladder note) but changes nothing
about serialization or ordering semantics anywhere a `Seed` was already
compared by `==`.

**The two required tests, and why each discriminates (not a stub).**
`windows/worldgen/tests/suite/weft_window.rs`:

- `the_window_is_byte_identical_to_no_window` — derives 200 walk-band
  facets (the same seed-42, vertex-0, "+a"-neighbor walk `weft_prevalence.rs`
  already measured non-degenerate: `occurs` fires 7/200, prevalence spans
  `[0.00071, 0.04641]`) two ways: directly via `prevalence`+`occurs`, and
  through a `WeftWindow` advanced one step at a time along the identical
  walk (radius 3, so real trailing/leading-edge churn happens along the
  way). Asserts the two `Vec<Vec<WeftFeature>>` are equal, THEN asserts
  non-vacuity independently (>=1 real occurrence, prevalence spread
  >=0.01) — so the equality can't be passing on a wall of empty vectors.
  **Verified to discriminate**: temporarily mutated `features_at` to always
  insert an empty `Vec` regardless of what was derived (a stand-in for "the
  cache silently drops what it derived") — both this test and the eviction

**Measured movement tax at radius 10 (seed 42, `Derived::misses()` deltas
per `advance_to` call, cold start excluded) — the plan's own arithmetic
(~84/step) was checked, not assumed, and did not survive the check:**

| movement | measured misses/step | formula |
|---|---:|---|
| straight-line edge step (any of the 4 edge directions, incl. direction changes among them) | **21** | `2·radius + 1` |
| diagonal step | **41** | `2·(2·radius+1) − 1` |
| cold start (first `advance_to` on an empty window) | 441 | `(2·radius+1)²` |

Also measured at radius 5 (straight-line: 11 = `2·5+1`) and radius 20
(straight-line: 41 = `2·20+1`), confirming the formula rather than resting
on one radius. **Why the plan's ~84 was wrong, not just imprecise:**
`84 / 21 = 4.0` exactly — the plan's arithmetic assumed a full ring
turnover every step (a `4·(2·radius+1) − 4`-shaped estimate), which is what
a step that changes BOTH axes at once (a diagonal) produces, not what a
walker moving in one direction produces. A player's actual walk is
straight-line-dominated (turns are diagonal only when the movement model
allows an eight-direction step and the player takes one), so **the real
per-step cost at radius 10 is ~4x cheaper than the plan's estimate for the
common case**, and exactly the plan's estimate's ballpark only on a
diagonal-heavy path. Measurement script: an `#[ignore]`d scratch test
(walked, measured via `println!`, transcribed above, then deleted — not
committed, since the record belongs here and in `window.rs`'s own doc, not
in a permanent perf test nobody asked for).

**What this dispatch didn't settle, left for Task 7/8:** `WeftFeature`'s
exact shape (`{kind, prevalence}`) is this task's own design choice, not
named in the brief beyond "`Vec<WeftFeature>`" — chosen because it's the
minimum a downstream reader (Task 8's walk-band line, Task 9's legibility
metric) needs without a second derivation call, and because `WeftKind` is
already `Copy` so carrying it in the value costs nothing. `features_at`
takes an explicit `kind` parameter (one (facet, kind) pair per call) rather
than returning every kind's features at once, matching `WeftKey` including
`kind` — a caller wanting "everything at this facet" loops
`WeftKind::ALL` itself (as `advance_to` does internally). No eligibility
filter was added (Task 5's known-open "59% of occurrences land on facets
with no cause, including open ocean" finding is explicitly Task 7's, per
the dispatch) — the window caches whatever `prevalence`/`occurs` says,
unfiltered, by design.
