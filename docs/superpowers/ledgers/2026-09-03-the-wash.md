# Decision ledger — The Wash (2026-09-03)

Campaign-autopilot ledger. Entries are written as rulings occur; the G3
package (spec review) digests everything up to that stop.

The Wash is the successor to [The Hachure](2026-09-02-the-hachure.md), which
fixed the map's *geometry* — the plate reads elevation between the mesh's
samples and draws rivers as lines. This campaign fixes its *ink*.

---

#1 [Q] — **Which piece of the map's colour problem is the next campaign?**
· Decision: **colour means biome, plus the channel algebra.** Nathan's call
from a four-way: this piece, over the biome taxonomy, over The Hachure's
deferred Stage 3, over the ocean.
· Why: the map is discarding data it already holds. Deferred Stage 3 would
have *invented* detail below the datum while the renderer was still failing
to show biome above it, which is the wrong order — you do not add
information to a channel that is throwing away the information it has.
· Alternatives discarded: Stage 3 as planned (`MAP-coherent-detail-field`,
still open); biome taxonomy depth first; ocean first. All three survive as
later campaigns and now have a channel to plug into.
· ideonomy passes: 0 for this framing question specifically — it was a
scope choice put to Nathan with four measured options, not a design
question. Passes were run on the design question it opened (#3, #4).
· Capture: this ledger; The Hachure's Stage 3 stays filed as
`MAP-coherent-detail-field`.

#2 [Q] — **The finding that occasioned it, verified twice.**
· `RELIEF_COLORS` is indexed by `relief_band(height)` — elevation. So is
`RELIEF_GLYPHS`. **Both channels carry the same quantity and biome carries
nothing**, while `domains/climate` holds a compositional
`BiomeExpr { realm, formation, stratum }` and `region.rs` already ships
`biome: Vec<u16>` plus a `biome_legend` over the wire.
· Consequence: a tropical rainforest and a high desert at the same elevation
render identically. Fixing it *frees* a channel rather than spending one.
· **Re-verified against `origin/main` at 939829db5**, after
`merge(the-prospect)` landed +1121 lines on `plate.rs` mid-brainstorm: all
four load-bearing facts survive — both ladders still six-wide and
band-indexed, `TileTerrain` still carries no biome,
`glyph_and_color_for(water, band)` unchanged. Recorded because the analysis
was first taken at `12612b1d1` and a stale read would have been invisible.
· ideonomy passes: 0 (a measurement).

#3 [G1] — **Where the Formation → hue grouping lives. ALL THREE OF MY
OPTIONS WERE OVERTURNED BY THE FIRST IDEONOMY PASS.**
· What I offered: (a) a visual grouping in `domains/climate`, (b) a
client-side table, (c) extend `hornvale_culture::BiomeClass`.
· What the pass found: **the question was wrong.** *Discovery-vs-invention*
splits the subject in two, and the halves have different homes:
  - **what a formation's ground is made of is DISCOVERED** — needle-leaf is
    dark blue-green, desert is pale, reef is bright. A world fact.
  - **the grouping into N hues is INVENTED**, and N is a property of the
    terminal (8, 16, 256, or zero under `NO_COLOR`). A display fact.
· Decision: **the sim owns a discovered cover/substance property; the client
owns the quantization to its own hue budget.**
· Why this is not a new principle: it is **quantize-at-emit-only**, the
kernel's existing constitutional discipline, applied to ink. The hue budget
IS a quantization and the client IS the emit boundary. Option (a) quantized
in the compute path; option (b) put a world fact in the renderer.
· It also dissolves what made (c) wrong, structurally rather than by taste:
`biome_class` collapses reef, kelp, open water, sea ice, ice, alpine and
caves into `Barren` — correct for "can people live off this", catastrophic
for "what does this look like". Cover does not collapse them, because they
are made of different stuff. The partition falls out of the world instead of
being chosen.
· Alternatives discarded: all three of my own options, with reasons above.
· ideonomy passes: 1 / overturns: 1. Tuple: cross-domain-reinstantiation +
negation, scale organon, prompts discovery-vs-invention / age / visibility.
The negation branch "not BY FORMATION → group by SUBSTANCE" is the one that
landed, and 0389 already says in terms that colour carries "category and
**substance**" — the 0389-native answer, which I had walked past. The
printing re-instantiation (separation and halftoning, never semantic
grouping) and the *age* prompt (a bronze-age mapmaker grouped terrain by
which pigments he owned) both converged on the same split independently.
· Capture: spec §; this is a ratification candidate.

#4 [G1] — **A second pass found a bigger structure than the first: colour is
a STACK AT DIFFERENT RATES, and the rate ladder is already the cache
ladder.**
· Occasioned by two constraints Nathan added mid-pass: (1) expanding biome
colours is **not** about conscious decoding — selecting a tile with the
cursor conveys the information — but about **aesthetics and perception of
variation**; (2) real-time aesthetic work is already live (the map marquee)
and is fair game so long as it does not touch the simulation.
· The first constraint dissolves the hue-budget argument the whole design
had been resting on. If colour is not the decoder, the budget is not a
recall budget, and **there are two cardinalities, not one**: a small
nameable legend-backed set that survives `NO_COLOR` and carries anything a
reader must trust, and a continuous unnameable ornamental modulation whose
absence costs nothing. That is **0389 satisfied rather than amended** — its
"nothing a reader must trust may live only in colour" constrains the first
layer and *licenses* the second.
· The second: colour layers sort by rate — geological (cover), seasonal
(snowline, leaf-off), diurnal (terminator), per-turn (the shipped synoptic
weather state), ornamental (~300 ms), instantaneous (cursor). **Three of
those rows have world data today and none is rendered.**
· And the ladder is not new machinery: decision 0289 already makes the map
layers with distinct cache keys, and those keys already sit at exactly these
rates — terrain never invalidated, perception per turn, marquee at 300 ms.
· Decision: **The Wash ships the trusted (geological) layer done right, plus
the layered-colour spine** — a documented rate→cache-layer contract so
seasonal, diurnal, weather and ornamental layers each have a defined slot.
It does **not** itself ship an ornamental layer.
· Alternatives discarded: trusted layer + one ornamental layer to prove the
spine (Nathan chose the narrower scope); spine + ornament first with the
biome correction deferred (rejected: the map would keep lying about biome
underneath prettier ornament).
· ideonomy passes: 1 / overturns: 1 (cumulative 2 / 2). Tuple:
organon-construction + dimension-identification, atlas organon, prompts
rate / cardinality / direction. *Rate* was the axis neither I nor the first
pass had surfaced. *Direction* surfaced a gap this campaign does NOT close:
currents, wind, flow and terminator sweep are vectors the world has and the
map has no channel for at all — recorded here so it is not rediscovered.
· Capture: spec §; the rate→cache contract is a ratification candidate.

#5 [PROC] — **I asserted the client had no frame loop and was wrong; Nathan
corrected it.**
· I wrote that the game client is turn-driven with "no frame loop and no
timer", and that a wave CA would therefore have "nothing to animate
against without inventing a clock". `clients/game/bin/src/main.rs:331`:

    const MARQUEE_TICK: Duration = Duration::from_millis(300);
    if driver.strip_is_scrolling() && !poll(MARQUEE_TICK)? { driver.tick_marquee(); }

· A poll-with-timeout that animates when the timeout expires, and only when
something wants animating — zero cost when nothing moves. The clock exists
and already has the shape the ornamental layer needs.
· **How I got it wrong:** I grepped `driver.rs` for `fn tick|frame|animat|
redraw|Duration::from_millis`, found only `mercator::Frame` (a projection
frame) and `redraw_count`, and reported the absence. The loop is in
`main.rs`, which I did not search. **A scoped grep is a claim about where
the thing lives**, and I had recorded that exact lesson from the merge-queue
operator ninety minutes earlier, in this session, and then committed the
same error.
· Capture: retrospective. The rule that would have caught it is the one
already written down — report the scope with the result, or search the
crate.

#6 [Q] — **Nathan expanded the vision: the sim should supply as much
visual-channel information as possible. Section 1 changes shape.**
· What he asked for: dirt colour, river colour, leaf colour in fall, snow
presence, hyperlocal moisture and temperature, grass browning in the dog
days, spring's new green, flower blooms, golden hour and sunrise and sunset,
lighting under different moon phases, luminescent bacteria in coastal
waters, aurorae. Plus: he wants the ornamental layer pursued soon, and
anything that does not touch the simulation is fair game.
· Decision: **Section 1 becomes an APPEARANCE VECTOR, not a closed `Cover`
enum.** The sim exposes named scalar axes; the client composes pixels. Never
an RGB value from the sim — that is pass 1's split (#3) held exactly, with
the sim's half made much larger than a five-value enum.
· Why this is not a new primitive: `kernel/src/ecology.rs` already has
`EnvironmentVector(BTreeMap<u16, f64>)` with named `EnvironmentAxis`
constants — open, scalar, deterministic, and already carrying `SUBSTRATE`
and `LIGHT`. Every item on Nathan's list is a named scalar over
(space × time), which is the kernel's own definition of a **Field**. Six of
the twelve are already modelled or directly derivable.
· **The correction this makes to my own framing:** I had proposed a closed
enum derived from `Formation`. That would have made every item on the list a
structural change instead of an additive one. The vector makes them
additive.
· Consequence: **there are TWO spines, not one.** The rate spine (client:
rate -> cache layer, where a LAYER may land) and the axis spine (sim: the
appearance vector and its registry, where a FACT may land).
· ideonomy passes: 0 — this was Nathan supplying a vision, not a question
put to me. The reframe it forced was mechanical once `EnvironmentVector` was
found.
· Capture: spec §; the axis spine is a ratification candidate alongside the
rate spine.

#7 [G2] — **Scope: both spines plus one axis per rate, rendered end to end.**
· Decision: cover/substrate (geological -> hue), snow_cover (seasonal),
sun_altitude (diurnal -> golden-hour tinting). Nathan's call, over the
narrower trusted-layer-only scope he had chosen before #6, and over a
client-only ornamental layer.
· Why the larger scope is defensible rather than ambitious: **feasibility
was verified before the scope was accepted, not after.** No new physics is
required anywhere:
  - `domains/astronomy/src/calendar.rs` already exposes `solar_altitude_at(t,
    latitude)`, `solar_azimuth_at`, `season_phase`, `daylight_fraction_at`,
    `is_daylight` and `moon_phase(t, index)`. The diurnal axis is a wrapper
    over shipped physics.
  - `domains/climate/src/temperature.rs` already exposes `temperature_at(...,
    vertex, day)` with obliquity, insolation, year phase and rotation regime,
    plus `seasonal_amplitude` and a diurnal amplitude map. Seasonal snow is
    derivable from shipped physics.
  - cover derives from `Formation`, which exists.
· So the campaign is composition, plumbing and rendering. The risk is in the
plate needing climate, astronomy and world time handed in, not in modelling.
· Alternatives discarded: trusted layer only (too little, given #6 and the
"soon"); one client-side ornamental layer (defers the axis spine's proof,
which is the half most likely to be wrong).
· ideonomy passes: 0 — a scope decision put to Nathan with measured
feasibility, following two passes on the design it scopes.
· Capture: spec §; the three axes are the campaign's stages.

#8 [G2] — **The invariant that keeps "it does not touch the simulation" true
by construction.**
· Decision: **appearance is DERIVED, NEVER COMMITTED, and never read back by
sim logic.** No appearance axis is written to the ledger, and nothing in
`domains/` or `windows/` may branch on one.
· Why: Nathan's licence for this work is that it does not affect the
simulation. A discipline that relies on nobody wiring it backwards will not
hold across campaigns. Making it structural means the licence stays true
without anyone remembering it.
· Precedent, exact in shape: decision 0346, an affordance is derived, never
committed. This is the same rule for the same reason applied to a different
derived quantity.
· ideonomy passes: 0 (an invariant read off an existing decision).
· Capture: spec §; ratification candidate.

#9 [G2] — **Roads, masonry and light sources: three shapes, and the design
only handled one.**
· Nathan asked how the design interacts with roads (dirt/cobble/gravel),
masonry, and lighting (torches, campfires, forest fires, volcanoes,
lightning). **None of it is modelled today** — verified by grep across
`domains/`, `windows/` and `kernel/`; only volcanism exists, as a terrain
and climate feature rather than a light source. So the question is whether
the architecture can ACCEPT them, which is the more useful form.
· The finding: there are **three shapes of appearance source**, and the
appearance vector as specced covers one.
  - **field** — cover, snow, sun altitude. Sampled per tile, dense.
  - **placed surface** — road, cobble, plaza. A sparse OVERRIDE on the dense
    default: `cover_at(t) = placed(t).unwrap_or(derived_cover(formation))`.
  - **emitter** — torch, campfire, wildfire, volcano, lightning. A source
    that CONTRIBUTES to neighbours with falloff. **The vector samples; it
    does not gather.**
· Decisions taken:
  - **Roads are LINES**, rendered by the polyline rasterizer The Hachure
    built under 0677, whose cover differs from the ground they cross. No new
    mechanism. This is the third instance of the linear-feature type (river,
    gallery forest, road), which suggests that rasterizer wants to be a
    general linear-feature renderer rather than a river one — recorded, not
    acted on.
  - **Masonry splits**: a horizontal built surface is a `cover` override; a
    vertical structure is a MARK, not a cover, because it occupies the tile
    rather than being the ground of it. Settlements are already drawn as
    marks. So masonry is mostly not a colour question.
  - **Lighting needs no change to the vector.** `LIGHT` is already an
    `EnvironmentAxis`. Ambient sources (sun, moon, aurora) WRITE it as a
    sampled field; emitters CONTRIBUTE to it by gathering. The client reads
    one scalar and cannot tell which produced it — which is **decision
    0003's own discipline**, that the channel does not carry a producer and
    a consumer receives appearances, never sources. The appearance vector is
    the Phenomena pattern applied to rendering. One axis, two composition
    rules.
  - **A new rung on the rate ladder: "built."** Roads and masonry change
    when something builds or destroys — settlement/history rate, slower than
    per-turn, faster than never. Without the rung a road is either baked into
    the never-invalidated cache (and survives its own destruction) or
    redrawn every turn for nothing.
  - **A torch is per-turn AND MOVING**, attached to a creature, so it can
    never live in the terrain cache; it belongs with the perception layer,
    which already redraws per turn. Recorded because it is the one case that
    does not sort cleanly by rate alone.
· **The omission, recorded with its reason** rather than papered over: the
axis spine covers FIELDS. Gathering is a second mechanism, The Wash does not
build it, and the spec says so in its non-goals. Reason: without that
sentence the next campaign finds the vector, assumes it covers torches, and
forces an emitter into a sampled shape — losing falloff and occlusion, and
producing a plausible result that is wrong.
· Net effect on scope: **none.** One extra rung, one explicit non-goal. That
all three questions landed on machinery that exists or is already planned is
weak evidence the spines are the right shape.
· ideonomy passes: 0 — this was a stress test of a design against three
supplied cases, not a choice among alternatives.
· Capture: spec §4 (the three shapes), §7 (non-goals).

#10 [G2] — **THE SPEC MIS-DIAGNOSED THE DEFECT AND WAS REWRITTEN. Nathan's
ECS question is what found it.**
· He asked whether to use or expand the ECS architecture for performance.
Following that into `kernel/` surfaced `kernel/src/color.rs` — **The
Pigment** — and the first draft of this spec collapsed.
· What exists, and what the spec proposed to build:

    proposed to build              already shipped
    ----------------------------------------------------------------
    a `cover` derivation           CoverClass { Bare, Chlorophyll,
                                   Litter, Snow, Sand, Silt }
                                   -- windows/locale/src/surface.rs
    a snow_cover axis              CoverClass::Snow
    sun_altitude -> "warmer tint"  astronomy::at_elevation(base,

                                   attenuate(illuminant, distance),

· **The defect is a missing CONSUMER, not missing data.** `git grep -c
'Reflectance|Illuminant|CoverClass|color::' clients/game/` returns **0**. The

six-entry hypsometric LUT that predates The Pigment.
· **And there is no scale gap**: `reflectance_at` takes a `Facet` and a
`WorldTime`; `cover_weights` takes a `Vertex` and a `WorldTime`.

· Decision: **rewrite the spec around consuming The Pigment.** Nathan chose
"rewrite, and check the vessel first" over rewriting immediately, and the

· **How I got it wrong, and it is the third instance today.** I searched

`kernel/src/color.rs`. A scoped search is a claim about where a thing lives.
The same error produced the marquee mistake (#5) and, earlier in the session,
a merge-queue peer's two failed greps. Three instances in one day, each
caught by someone else or by a later search rather than by review.
· ideonomy passes: 0 — a factual correction against shipped code, not a
choice among alternatives. The two earlier passes' conclusions SURVIVE and
were strengthened: pass 1's discovered/invented split is exactly The

· Capture: spec rewritten in full; §1 opens with the mis-diagnosis rather

#11 [G2] — **Where the ECS substrate earns its place, which is not where I
would have put it.**
· Nathan asked for ECS "if appropriate". It is appropriate, in one specific
place, and the reason is the rate spine rather than the entity model.
· The naive implementation recomputes the illuminant for all ~4,800 tiles of
a plate. **Hoisting it out of the per-tile loop is the whole optimisation**:
the illuminant is diurnal and uniform across the plate, only reflectance
varies per tile.
· Per-tile reflectance is a derived value keyed by facet — which is what
`kernel/src/component.rs`'s `ComponentStore<K: Ord, C>` and
`kernel/src/derived.rs` exist for. `derived.rs` is one generic store per
value SHAPE, deliberately not heterogeneous, because `TypeId`'s ordering is
not build-stable and a `BTreeMap<TypeId, _>` would put an unstable iteration
order under a byte-identity guarantee.
· So the cache is keyed `(Facet, season)` and invalidated on the season
boundary. **That key falls out of the rate spine**, not out of ECS thinking:
`reflectance_at` takes a `WorldTime`, so reflectance is seasonal-rate, and
the terrain layer's cache is never invalidated. Putting reflectance in the
terrain layer unchanged is precisely the bug the rate invariant names.
· **The invariant I invented for hypothetical future layers turns out to be
load-bearing for stage one.** Recorded because that is unusual enough to be
worth distrusting: it is either a good sign the spine is real, or a sign I
fitted the invariant to the case I had. The plan should test it red-first.
· ideonomy passes: 0.
· Capture: spec §4.5, §6 (H4).

#12 [G4] — **Plan self-review found one real coverage gap and two labelling
gaps.**
· Ran the writing-plans self-review against the spec's five hypotheses. H1,
H2 and H3 were covered and named. **H4 was covered but unnamed** (Task 1 IS
the rate invariant, red-first) and **H5 was genuinely uncovered.**
· The H5 gap is the interesting one, because it is a shape this project has
a name for. Task 2 asserted "the None observer renders no colour" and Task 6
asserted "the glyph still carries elevation order" — two true halves that do
not add up to the claim. **H5 is the CONJUNCTION**: under `NO_COLOR` the
plate emits no colour AND still distinguishes relief. Either half alone is
satisfiable by a degenerate implementation — a plate emitting no colour and
no distinction is "degraded" into uselessness and passes the first; a plate
that never degrades passes the second.
· Decision: added `a_no_colour_plate_emits_no_colour_and_still_shows_relief`
to Task 6, asserting both arms over one drawn plate, and labelled every task
with the spec hypothesis it discharges.
· Why this matters beyond the fix: **two tests that each assert half of a
claim read exactly like coverage of the whole claim**, and the plan's task
structure actively hides it, because the halves live in different tasks with
different reviewers. The self-review caught it only because it walked the
SPEC's list rather than the plan's.
· ideonomy passes: 0 (a checklist finding, not a design choice).
· Capture: plan Task 6; retrospective — this is the reusable half.
