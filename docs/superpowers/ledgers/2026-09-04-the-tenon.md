# The Tenon — decision ledger

Campaign C of the `MAP-one-kind-model` arc: **kind-to-kind edges**, with the
sleep-site preference as the named consumer.

Decision block reserved: **0726–0735** (`make decision-block NAME=the-tenon`,
main ceiling 0698 at reservation).

#1 [Q] — **Campaign name: The Tenon.** A tenon is the projecting half of a
mortise-and-tenon joint: it holds because two pieces were each cut to suit the
other, and *neither piece names the other* — which is exactly the M+N property
the chosen edge shape has. `tenon` has zero occurrences anywhere in the tree
(`git grep -il tenon` → no matches), and no `the-tenon` entry exists in
`docs/retrospectives/` or `book/src/chronicle/`. `the-mortise` and `the-scarf`
were both checked and are taken (2 files each). No ideonomy pass — a name is
not a design decision.

#2 [G1] — **Scope: this campaign adds sleepable kinds AND the edge, and the
epoch is accepted.** Nathan's call, brought to him under the brief's explicit
instruction (a fidelity/scope decision) rather than resolved from precedent.

The evidence assembled for the call, all measured rather than reasoned:

- `the-fireside-bed` is the sole `ObjectProperty::SupportsRest` carrier
  (`windows/vessel/src/affordance.rs`, `object_registry`), and it requires
  `built && cold` at both bands.
- `cold-built-room-share`: median **0.186**, mean **0.262** over n=1000 worlds
  (`book/src/domesday/settlement.md`). So ~81% of built rooms, and **100% of
  wild rooms**, contain nothing to sleep on at all.
- The wild band has exactly **two** patterns, `the-clearing` and `the-pool`
  (`windows/vessel/src/interior/pattern.rs`, `INVENTORY`); neither is
  sleepable.
- Whether any body in any world has ever taken an `Afforded` bout is
  **unmeasured** — decision 0697 records that the affect-trace byte-golden is
  blind to the afforded path because seed 42's traced window is entirely open
  ground, and `PSY-rest-site-is-a-tuning-indicator` records the reachability
  test as unbuilt.

Discarded: *kinds first as a separate campaign* (front-loads the epoch, but
splits one fidelity story across two merges); *mechanism only* (ships an
unexercised layer on an unexercised layer — the exact compounding The Pallet
warned against).

Ideonomy: 1 pass, 1 overturn — see #3, which is the same pass.

#3 [G1] — **Edge shape: derived from traits both sides carry (form B), not an
authored `(species, thing)` matrix and not a general typed graph. OVERTURN.**
Nathan's call, presented with a recommendation; the recommendation itself was
produced by the pass and is not what I came in holding.

Tuple drawn: operators `abstraction-lift` + `substitution`, organon `notation`,
dimension-prompts `distribution`, `cardinality`, `symmetry`. **One pass, one
overturn.**

**What I was going to recommend:** the frontier essay's stated target — a
general typed graph over `KindId` (`EdgeKind = RestsOn | GrowsOn | SeedsBecome`),
with sleep as its first consumer.

**What the pass produced.**

The `distribution` substitution priced a new sleepable kind under each form:
**39 rows** under an authored matrix, **1 row** under a derived function. The
arc's own thesis is that adding a kind must be cheap — The Wicket's proof was a
brazier reaching a real world through five data rows and no dispatcher edit —
so an authored matrix *contradicts the campaign arc it belongs to*. Measured
against the tree to check the house idiom rather than assume it: **95**
`ComponentStore<KindId, _>` tables exist under `domains/*/src` and
`windows/*/src`, and **zero** pair-keyed tables anywhere outside test code
(`grep '(KindId, KindId)'` hits only `interior/pattern.rs`'s own census test).
`ObjectProperty::Lockable`'s doc states the rule outright: *"Still M+N: the
lock declares what it requires and the key declares what it carries, and
neither names the other."*

The `abstraction-lift` found the comparator that settles the false dichotomy.
Lifted, this is *a bipartite compatibility relation between two open,
string-keyed classes*. Blood-type compatibility is an 8×8 table everyone
memorizes as a matrix and which two three-bit antigen vectors generate
entirely; matrix factorization makes the general statement — **an authored M×N
matrix of rank r IS r latent traits per side.** So "table or function" is not
an architecture choice, it is a choice of *rank*, and the question becomes what
rank this world needs. The project has already answered a structurally
identical question once: Pianka niche overlap in the coexistence packer
computes a pairwise compatibility from two `ResourceVector`s rather than
authoring it.

The `cardinality` substitution killed my own recommendation. A general graph
with one live `EdgeKind` is speculative generality, and `ObjectProperty`'s own
doc refuses exactly that shape: *"each variant exists because a shipped or
imminent verb gates on it, and a property no verb would ever consult was cut
rather than kept."*

The `symmetry` prompt surfaced the slot nothing in the tree declares, and it is
the reason form C would have been wrong rather than merely early: the arc's own
exemplars disagree about direction. `grows-on` is anti-symmetric, `family_of`
(the frontier essay's "degenerate case") is symmetric-transitive, and
*rests-on* is neither — the **fit** is symmetric (two shapes suiting each
other) while the **payoff** is asymmetric (only the sleeper gains). One
undirected `edges` abstraction would silently merge all three.

**And form B dissolves the layering problem the brief called the campaign's
sharpest structural question.** Under B no crate ever names both rosters: one
side carries surface traits, `domains/species` already carries the sleeper's
axis (`sleep_grade_registry`, decision 0697), and the combining function lives
in the consumer naming neither roster's keys. There is no
sibling-domain dependency to justify and no composition-root exception to
claim.

**What form B costs, stated rather than glossed:** *a dwarf prefers stone* must
fall out of traits; it cannot be asserted. A pair the traits get wrong has no
override. Nathan was shown that cost and chose B over `B + sparse override`,
whose second source of truth for one question is the exact disagreement
`domains/thing`'s own module doc records deleting a field to prevent.

Alternatives discarded, with reasons: **A** (authored matrix) — 39 rows per new
kind, and the catalogue-of-solutions shape `interior/pattern.rs` explicitly
refuses; **B + override** — two tables answering one question; **C** (typed
graph) — one abstraction, one live consumer.

Capture: form C is not dead, it is deferred with its blocking condition now
named (a second `EdgeKind` with a live consumer). An idea-registry row records
that, and the `symmetry` finding, at G3.

said the surface traits would live in **`domains/thing`**, on a symmetry

Two corrections were then made to the entry above — the `ComponentStore` count

The G2 self-review then found a second, sharper reason, recorded as #4.

**Nothing about the G1 ruling changes**: form B still holds, and the layering
property that justified it — *no crate ever names both rosters* — is

#4 [G2] — **The surface offer lives in the row its marker lives in, because
the alternative is a structure that has already drifted in this exact
codebase.**

The obvious shape is a second `KindId`-keyed table: `ObjectProperty::
SupportsRest` stays a marker in `object_registry`, and a sibling
`rest_surface_registry` carries the scalar. That is what the second ideonomy
pass was run against, and the tree-walk across `ObjectProperty`'s siblings
found the shape already built — and already broken.

`ObjectProperty::RadiatesHeat` has **two** carriers (`hearth`, `brazier`). Its
scalar lives in `warmth_at` (`windows/vessel/src/interior/field.rs:73`) as a
literal `if interior.anchor(id).kind != kinds::HEARTH { continue; }` — **one**
carrier. The marker table grew and the scalar did not follow.
`object_registry`'s own doc records the consequence as live: *"no other kind
contributes a degree of warmth however many rows this table grows."*

Verified rather than recalled: `field.rs:73` reads exactly that today.

So `RestSurface` is a **field on `ObjectTraits`**, with a **two-way** agreement
invariant (marker ⇒ row and row ⇒ marker). One-directional would be blind to
precisely the half that broke `RadiatesHeat`, and would still read as total to
the next reader — the failure The Wicket's six registry checks were written to
name.

Alternatives discarded: a second table with a one-way check (rebuilds the
observed defect); attaching a payload to the `ObjectProperty` variant itself
(it is `Copy` and lives in a `BTreeSet`, so two rest surfaces with different
values would become distinct set members — a correctness break, not a style
one).

Ideonomy: 1 pass. Tuple `tree-finding` + `organon-construction`, organon
`scale`, prompts `source`, `visibility`, `hierarchicalness`. No overturn; this
finding is the pass's product — walking *across* to `ObjectProperty`'s siblings
is what surfaced `RadiatesHeat`, and I would not have looked at it otherwise.

#5 [G2] — **The rank is 2, and both bounds are forced rather than chosen.**

Same pass; the `scale` organon was built over *rank*.

**Lower bound.** A rank-1 model (species scalar × surface scalar) is
*separable* — nothing about the pair is stored anywhere — so it is two
independent kind-level components, of which the tree already has 95. An edge
carries information absent from either endpoint alone, which begins at rank 2.
The campaign's own title therefore forbids rank 1.

**Upper bound.** `ObjectProperty`'s "minimal and earned" doc cuts any axis no
consumer reads. A third contrast has none today.

**And the `source` prompt killed the additive model I had queued first.** The
shipped ladder is not additive — `CONTACT_ONLY` 1.20 + insulation step 0.15 +
fit step 0.10 = 1.45, against a both-halves value of **1.50** — so an additive
reconstruction needs a `+0.05` interaction constant whose only honest source is
an author balancing a table. Decision 0586 refuses exactly that answer; a
`plumb:` reason for it could only say where the number came from, the
neighbouring question 0697 was itself minted to correct. The multiplicative
form carries no such constant, and reproduces the bed column byte for byte by
construction (spec §5.2).

#6 [G2] — **The recovery fold reads `SLEPT_ON`, which decision 0698 built and
left unread on purpose.**

0698: *"Nothing reads the predicate yet, deliberately… Grading a body's outcome
on which kind it found is the `per-people` rung 0697 defers."* This campaign is
that rung, so it is the reader. `rest_timeline` already resolves the site by
one ordered merge against the position timeline in `O(bouts + positions)`; a
second merge against the entity's `SLEPT_ON` facts is the same cursor idiom.

The room-level fallback stays for bouts with no such fact — `Action::Rest`
bouts and every pre-Pallet ledger — and deleting it would silently mis-grade
committed history. No ideonomy pass: 0698 answers this outright and names this
campaign as the answer's consumer.

#7 [G2] — **The motivating sentence changes actor: drow, not dwarf, and this
is a finding rather than a wording choice.**

*A dwarf sleeps better on stone than on straw* is a **reversal**, and no
derived model over today's traits can produce one for dwarves.
`habitat_realm_registry` is sparse and holds exactly **three** rows —
`rust-monster`, `xorn`, `drow` — and its own comment records that The Delvers
"briefly added two subterranean PEOPLES here and withdrew them." Nothing else
separates `hill-dwarf` from `human`: both `Endothermic`, both `Settled`, both
under a tonne, both `MADE_FOR_THE_BODY`.

The roster supplies the same sentence with `drow`, whose subterranean row its
own comment calls "Drow's ONLY authored separation from the surface elves…
deliberately its only one" — authored so a mechanism could find it. Verified
reachable rather than assumed: `book/src/gallery/settlement-seed-42.md` reads
*"The chief drow settlement, Poogpao, holds 25 souls amid temperate-forest."*

Discarded: authoring a dwarf substrate axis to rescue the original phrasing —
a new species axis whose only consumer is this campaign, which is the shape
`ObjectProperty`'s own rule cuts. **Flagged for G3**: it changes the headline
example Nathan wrote. No ideonomy pass — this is a measurement, not a choice.

#8 [G2] — **The chooser prefers, and the ability to choose badly is preserved
by the no-travel rule rather than by the tie-break.**

`select_sleep_site`'s doc warns that a future chooser "must preserve a body's
ability to end up on a worse site" and that one "*guaranteed* to find the
optimum" would erase the tuning signal. Within-room argmax **is** guaranteed to
find the room's optimum, so the letter is not satisfied.

Its purpose is, by the rule stated directly above it in the same doc: the
chooser never proposes movement and never looks past this room's anchors. A
body sleeps on bracken while a bed stands in the next room — which is exactly
Nathan's *"a creature sleeping in an unsafe or unrestful place would be a
useful indicator that something needs tuning."*

Recorded as a decision rather than resolved inside a doc comment, because it
**amends a warning The Pallet wrote deliberately**. **Flagged for G3.** No
ideonomy pass: the question is whether a stated purpose survives a stated
mechanism, which is a reading, not a design space.
