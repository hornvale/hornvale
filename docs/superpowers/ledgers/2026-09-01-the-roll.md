## Brainstorm (pre-G3)

### #1 [G1] — how do we get from seven bodies to thousands?

**Question:** Three approaches were on the table. (A) Turn the dial: raise
`NPC_COUNT`/`WILD_COUNT` and derive `population` bodies per settlement for
every settlement. (B) A resident tier plus a working set: every settlement
carries `population` residents derived on demand by lineage; the walk ticks
only those within call of the observer, a pure function of (observer room,
`WorldTime`, seed). (C) Cut the per-agent constant first, then turn the dial.

**Decision:** (B), with (C)'s levers folded in as one stage (spec §6).

**Why (precedent cited):** (A) is arithmetic: ~7,400 inhabitants × 8.76 ms
per agent-tick (Penstock metaplan §6.4, post-Scour) is ~65 s per tick.
(C) alone cannot reach it either — a 5× cut leaves 13 s. (B) is the
Penstock metaplan's own spine (§2: "the condensed set is dozens to low
thousands of real entities, derived on demand … everything else is a field
sample, never instantiated and never ticked"), The Walk §4.1's refinement
protocol, and the ECS metaplan §8's "lazy and observer-relative — game as
lens." The Hand's ruling (retro §"An equality proof licenses a deletion
only on the axis it measured") gave the population question its own
campaign; this is it. Identity by lineage through `reuse_or_mint_entity`
(0051, 0127) is already built for the re-derivation case.

**Alternatives discarded:** (A) on cost; (C)-alone on cost; residents
minted at genesis into the world ledger — see #2.

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 2 / 0. Pass 1 (dimension-identification +
abstraction-lift, periodic-grid; prompts polarity, size, source): the grid
of existence-tier × membership-source showed the *entity* tier and the
*body* tier are separate axes — a resident can exist (be named, hold a
thing) without being ticked — which became spec §3's three-tier shape; the
polarity flip ("the dark version of more creatures") surfaced presence-at-
scale rendering, spec §4, which nothing in the brief had raised; the
abstraction lift showed founders and residents are one kind at two times
(#3). Pass 2 (substitution, spectrum; prompts rate, longevity): the rate
axis exposed the unnamed middle between frozen and ticked — a slow tier —
captured as a registry row and not built (#4); longevity gave residents a
drawn age (spec §3.4). Pass 2 produced no change to the approach itself;
called converged.

**Capture:** spec §3, §4, §10; registry row `SOC-off-roll-slow-tier`.

### #2 [Q] — are residents generated at genesis or derived on demand?

**Question:** Should a settlement's residents be minted into the world
ledger by worldgen (an epoch: new entities, new facts, keystone golden
moves, census rebaseline) or derived on demand into the session ledger?

**Decision:** Derived on demand, into the session ledger, by lineage.

**Why:** Penstock §2 ("derived on demand and cached"); decision 0227
(possession selects a body, it does not mint one — bodies are already
session-derived); the census cost structure (metric extraction is 95% of a
census, and every metric scanning facts would pay for ~7,400 new entities
× several facts on 1000 worlds); and "sim first" is satisfied either way,
since the derivation is a pure function of the world any window may call.
Decision 0050 (entity-hood for collections) points the other way on its
face but governs *genesis* collections; the later, more specific Penstock
text governs here.

**Alternatives discarded:** genesis minting (epoch, census cost, and it
answers a question — who lives here — for 264 settlements the player will
never visit).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — the periodic grid in #1's pass 1
examined exactly this cell (entity × {world state, observer}) and found both
are needed: the *count* is world state, the *minting* is observer-triggered.

**Capture:** spec §3.1, §2.1.

### #3 [Q] — is a resident a person?

**Question:** Do residents reuse `domains/person`'s vocabulary
(`is-person`, `person-born`) or get their own?

**Decision:** Reuse it. A resident is a living person; a founder is a dead
one.

**Why:** "One word per concept" (memory: one-word-per-concept-beats-
audience-carve-outs; decision 0025's one-owner rule for names);
`PSY-expertise-per-individual` (elaborated) already says per-person
components belong where `domains/person` is "a seed plus an empty marker
struct"; and `SOC-person-death-unrendered` records that no window reads
`person-born`/`person-died` — giving those predicates a living population
is the cheapest route to a reader. The session ledger is disjoint from the
world fixture, so worldgen tests and lab metrics that count founders are
untouched.

**Alternatives discarded:** a `resident` predicate family (a second word
for the same concept); no predicates at all (a resident that no tool can
query is lens-owned state, against principle 1).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — #1's abstraction lift ("founders
and residents are the same shape at different times") is the pass.

**Capture:** spec §3.1.

### #4 [Q] — what happens to a body off the roll?

**Question:** Frozen at its last committed facts and caught up on return;
a slow tier (one decision per day off-roll); or advanced by the field?

**Decision:** Frozen + `catch_up`, the mechanism sleeping bodies already
use (`liveness.rs:4632`, `CATCH_UP_STEP_CAP`). **This is a fidelity choice
and leads the G3 flagged section.**

**Why:** The Walk §4.1 — refinement is "stored only if it is an irreversible
divergence"; a dormant resident's divergence is exactly its committed
facts. The slow tier is a second visible mechanism with its own purity
obligations and its own cost (7,400 × one decision per world-day is cheap
per tick but is a new term), and it is not needed to answer the brief.
Field advancement contradicts nothing but has no field to advance a
*position* by.

**Alternatives discarded:** slow tier (captured, not built); field
advancement (no mechanism).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — #1's pass 2 (rate, spectrum)
named the slow tier as the spectrum's unnamed middle.

**Capture:** spec §3.7; registry row `SOC-off-roll-slow-tier`.

### #5 [Q] — does the key move onto a resident?

**Question:** The registry row instructs "whoever raises it: a key waits to
move onto a person." Move it?

**Decision:** No, with the reason recorded (spec §7): no verb transfers a
held thing between creatures and no drive sets one down, so the move makes
the only lock unopenable — decision 0398 (a capability nothing can reach is
not a capability), 0516 beside it.

**Alternatives discarded:** move it and add a transfer verb (a second
campaign's worth of grammar); move it and add a "drops on sleep" drive (a
mechanism invented to rescue a placement).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — a substitution pass over the
holder axis (floor / container / creature / nowhere) found "creature" is
the only value with no reachability path today; the pass changed nothing
and confirmed the deferral.

**Capture:** spec §7; `PLAY-key-placement-stands-in-for-a-resident` Where
cell; retrospective follow-up.

### #6 [Q] — are residents named, and are wild bodies?

**Question:** Eighty bodies labelled "goblin of Googo" collapse in the
chart legend and in `examine`. Name them?

**Decision:** Residents: yes, by `hornvale_language::Namer` — the founder
path's own namer (`person_promote.rs:316`), seeded per resident. Wild: no;
grouped in prose.

**Why:** Founders are already named this way (precedent); a label is what
`examine` matches, so distinctness is a correctness requirement, not
flavour. Wild members are interchangeable and a herd is a herd.

**Alternatives discarded:** ordinal suffixes ("goblin 3 of Googo") — a
name a world would never produce; naming wild bodies — no reader wants
"Elk 7".

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — #1's polarity flip is the pass
that raised the requirement.

**Capture:** spec §3.5, §4.

### #7 [Q] — is individuation in scope?

**Question:** Ship residents as identical copies and leave
`PSY-individual-deviation` for later, or draw per-resident deviation now?

**Decision:** In scope, bounded to the four authored dials a `Body` carries
(spec §3.3), with a preregistered visibility measurement (M5) whose null is
a finding.

**Why:** Nathan's Wicket ruling ("should actually be a component … and
might have personal preferences as well"); memory: prefer heavyweight
campaigns, the fixed toll punishes small ones; and a settlement of eighty
copies is the brief satisfied in number and not in kind.

**Alternatives discarded:** copies now, deviation later (two tolls for one
change); deriving *which* axes a species has (`PSY-axes-from-ecology`) — a
different question.

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — #1's pass 1 dimension
"homogeneity" (identical / drawn / accumulated / authored) placed the
campaign at "drawn + accumulated" and nothing in pass 2 moved it.

**Capture:** spec §3.3, §8 M5; `PSY-individual-deviation` → elaborated.

### #12 [Q] — wild identity

**Decision:** A wild body is keyed by (species, attractor vertex, member
index); `derive_wild_npcs` (keyed by top-k list position) stays for the lab
and the benches. Spec §3.2 amended.

**Why:** Keying by list position would give a herd a different identity
depending on which direction the player approached from — a working set
that is not a function of world state (Penstock §2b).

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — symmetry-hunting: "the same herd
from the east and from the west" must be one herd.

### #13 [Q] — roll membership by home or by position?

**Decision:** By home (the settlement's room or the herd's attractor).

**Why:** A body's current position is its own history; its home is world
state. A resident who wandered out of the window while its settlement is in
call is still ticked, which is both cheaper (no per-body position read to
decide membership) and the purer function.

**Ideonomy passes / overturns:** 1 / 0 — substitution on the membership
axis (home / position / either / both); "either" doubles the walk's reads
for no fidelity a player can see.
