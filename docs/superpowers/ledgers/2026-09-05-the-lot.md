# The Lot — decision ledger

Campaign: **The Lot** — draw one life from everyone who ever lived in a
seeded world, and tell it from the ledger alone.
Opened 2026-09-05 from Nathan's one-line brief: *"We should be able to do
something very much like this: https://anyhumanever.com"*.
Branch: `campaign/the-lot`. Decision block: **0796–0805** (reserved
2026-09-05 via `make decision-block`; main ceiling 0750 at reservation).

A *lot* is the thing chance draws, and a person's lot is the life they were
dealt. Both senses are the campaign.

## The source, read once

Any Human Ever (anyhumanever.com) draws one life from the >100 billion humans
who have ever lived, in four staged reveals — **When** (a birth year drawn off
the population curve, log-scaled because almost everyone was born recently),
**Where** (a place drawn off a HYDE population map for that year), **Life**
(the demographic odds at that people/place/year: life expectancy, died before
5 / 15, causes of death, marriage and children — then a timeline of one life
drawn from those odds), **Story** (a templated biography with ~25 stat tiles,
each tile and sentence carrying its sources). Its rules, from its own source
and `/sources` page: one seed pre-rolls the whole human (permalink = seed);
the story is *"created from the statistical reality of <place> in <year>"*
and says so above the text; a tile is **absent, never invented**, where no
source covers the region and era ("an invented figure would be worse than
silence"); every source carries a provenance label (`extracted` /
`attributed` / `authored` / `mixed`); the only AI in it is an optional
portrait, off by default. The author's stated thesis: history is *"a
statistician's nightmare"* — most lives ever lived were *"Asian, poor, and
brief"*, and a random draw shows you that where a curated anecdote cannot.

## Entries

#1 [G1] — **What is the Hornvale analogue of "draw one human from everyone
who ever lived"?** ·
**Decision: a *lot* — one representative life drawn from a seeded world's
person-years, told from committed facts alone, silent wherever the ledger
is silent, and never itself committed.** Delivered as (a) a window,
`windows/lot`, that turns `(World, LotIndex)` into a `Life` and renders it
as prose and as a provenance-tagged JSON payload; (b) `hornvale lot` on the
CLI; (c) a committed, drift-checked gallery page of drawn lives for seed 42;
(d) a browser exhibit in the book — the four-stage reveal — over a
`world-wasm` ABI addition; and (e) a preregistered readout of what the draw
population looks like and how much of a life the ledger can currently tell. ·
Why: the site's own rule is *"depicted as well as the data allow"*, and that
is exactly `windows/explain`'s contract — narrate from the ledger, never the
in-memory system, *because* that validates the ledger is sufficient. A slot
the story cannot fill is therefore not a rendering gap but a measured
absence in the world's own record, which turns the lens into an instrument.
Precedent: decision 0022 (the sim renders nothing; a client is free), 0055
(a versioned wasm catalog is how a browser reaches a world), the Casement
(`book/src/gallery/possession-live.md`: a wasm exhibit in the book), the
explain window (ledger-only narration), `windows/worldgen/src/descent.rs`
("nothing here is committed" — individuals derived on demand from the
committed community tree), `domains/history::flesh::persona_of` (a persona is
a pure hash over `(handle, seed)`, no stream drawn). ·
Alternatives discarded: (i) **a text-only lens with the exhibit deferred** —
splits one idea across two campaigns for no attribution gain, and the
exhibit cannot move sim prose, so sequencing inside one campaign already
gives attribution (memory: prefer heavyweight campaigns); (ii) **a lives
corpus** in the `tropes/` `systems/` `sentences/` family — authored lives as
demands on the ledger — is a real idea but a different instrument (a corpus
is authored data; a lot is drawn from the world), captured as a registry row
rather than built; (iii) **committing drawn lives as person entities** —
would make an observation into world-state, a save-format surface for a
value the seed re-derives exactly, the argument `Community::rung`'s doc
already makes; (iv) **an LLM-written story** — forbidden by *models author,
dice roll* (decision 0009); the site itself uses no model for the text. ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: **1 pass, 0 overturns, 4 enrichments** — the
organon is recorded under *Ideonomy* below. ·
Capture: this entry; registry rows (see manifest); spec.

#2 [Q] — **The ledger keeps no population trajectory (only `founded`,
`ended`, `peak`). What does the birth-year draw weight on?** ·
**Decision: commit one new functional Number fact per occupation,
`occ-person-years` — the integral of the community's live population over
its tenure, tallied during the bake — and reconstruct the *within-tenure*
shape from `(founded, ended, peak, person-years)`.** Between occupations the
weight is then exact; within one it is a shape with the right area and
peak. ·
Why: this is precisely the pattern `Occupation::delve_depth_m` already
argues for in its own doc comment — "the working is the integral of a live
quantity over a tenure … the ledger keeps neither trajectory … so the field
commits exactly the half nothing can re-derive." A per-epoch series
(alternative a) would be ~20k Number facts per world for a quantity whose
median tenure is two epochs; an approximation from `tenure × peak` alone
(alternative b) over-weights every community that died young and never
reached its peak for long, which the probe's `peak==0` and short-tenure
counts will size. A new fact is **additive, draws nothing, and moves no
seeded value** — "new draws are the only epoch-triggering additions"
(`windows/CLAUDE.md`) — but every saved world's JSON grows by one fact per
occupation, so the goldens and the census regenerate. Flagged for G3 as
save-format-adjacent. ·
Alternatives discarded: (a) per-epoch series (size, and a path nobody else
reads); (b) `tenure × peak` (biased, and the bias is toward exactly the
lives the lens is for — the short-lived communities); (c) re-running the
bake at draw time to recover the trajectory — a window reading the
in-memory system, the thing `windows/CLAUDE.md` names as the weakest shape. ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (substitution on the *distribution*
axis: what if the weight lived in the reader instead of the ledger? — that
is (c), rejected), 0 overturns. ·
Capture: spec §4.1; G3 flagged item.

#3 [Q] — **Births need a birth rate. Which?** ·
**Decision: a stationary-population rate, births per person-year =
1 / e₀, where e₀ is the life expectancy at birth the campaign's own
mortality model (#4) yields for that people at that era.** So the two halves
agree by construction: a people the model makes short-lived contributes more
births per person-year, exactly as the site's era crude-birth-rate table
does for the human past. ·
Why: no fertility model exists in the tree (`domains/species::allometry` has
`reproductive_tempo` and `age_at_maturity` but no births-per-female);
stationarity is the one closed-form assumption that needs no new authored
number, and the bake's own logistic growth toward capacity makes most
tenures near-stationary in fact (to be confirmed by the probe). ·
Alternatives discarded: a per-species authored crude birth rate (a new
authored number with no measurement behind it — The Plumb's default-deny
would rightly ask for its provenance); births ∝ person-years / generation
length (ignores mortality, so a people that loses half its children before
maturity would be under-drawn as children — the opposite of the lens's
point). ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (inversion: weight by *deaths* instead of
births — equivalent under stationarity, and it is what makes "everyone who
ever lived" and "everyone who ever died" the same population up to the
living), 0 overturns. ·
Capture: spec §4.2.

#4 [Q] — **There is no mortality model. What does a life's length come
from?** ·
**Decision: a Siler hazard — infant (falling exponential) + background
(constant) + senescent (Gompertz, rising exponential) — with its age scale
set by the people's allometric lifespan (`hornvale_species::allometry::
lifespan`, anchored at 60 years for a 40 kg endotherm) and its background
term modulated by the era's tech horizon and the site's strife field
(`hornvale_demography::strife`). The community's own committed fate — an
occupation that ended by famine, plague, burning, breach, flight or
migration inside the life's span — is a *discrete* hazard on top: the life
either ends in it (with probability the bake's own loss constants imply:
`WAR_LOSS` 0.3, `1 − MIGRATE_SURVIVAL` 0.1) or continues in the daughter
community the record names.** The parameter values are frozen in the spec
before any life is drawn (decision 0016). ·
Why: Siler is the standard three-component model of a whole-life mortality
curve and is what the site's own life tables (Coale–Demeny West, HMD) are
smoothings of; scaling it by allometric lifespan is the only way to make a
kobold's and a bugbear's curves differ from one measured input rather than
four authored ones. The discrete community-fate hazard is where Hornvale is
*richer* than the site: the catastrophe that struck a life is a committed
event with a cause, a date and a successor, not a regional frequency table. ·
Alternatives discarded: a bare Gompertz (no infant mortality — and the
site's "died before 5 / 15" tiles are the whole thesis); authored per-era
life tables (authored numbers, no provenance); deriving e₀ from the bake's
growth rate (the bake has no deaths, only net logistic growth). ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (cardinality: one hazard curve per
people-era vs one per *community* — the strife field is per vertex, so the
curve IS per community already; enrichment, not overturn), 0 overturns. ·
Capture: spec §4.3; fidelity item for G3 (this is a modelling choice with
accuracy consequences, and the carve-out says Nathan sees those).

#5 [Q] — **Where does the draw's randomness live? A window may not draw
world-state.** ·
**Decision: a lot is an *observation*, not a fact. Its randomness is the
reader's: `LotIndex(u64)` is an input like a world seed, and every choice in
the life is a pure hash-expansion of `(world.seed, index, choice-label)` in
the style of `persona_of` — no kernel `Stream`, no label in any
`streams.rs`, no manifest entry, nothing committed. The permalink is
`<seed>/<index>`.** ·
Why: `windows/CLAUDE.md`'s rule exists because a window that draws "has
quietly become a domain with no registry entry and no pin-isolation test" —
a domain in disguise *commits*. A lot commits nothing and moves no saved
world, so the hazard the rule guards is absent; and a `Stream` label would
promise a save-format contract for a value that is not saved. `persona_of`
is the exact precedent: "bit arithmetic over the arguments alone … trivially
total and deterministic". The same `(seed, index)` will draw the same life
until the ledger it reads changes — which is the right shelf life for an
observation. ·
Alternatives discarded: a `lot/life` stream label at the composition root
(a save-format contract for nothing saved); committing lots as `is-person`
entities (#1 (iii)). ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (autonomy: who drives the draw — the
reader or the world? Splitting the axis produced the *four kinds of
individual* dictionary entry below and the "possess a lot" bridge captured
as a registry row), 0 overturns. ·
Capture: spec §3; decision record 0796 ("a lot is an observation, not a
fact"); registry row PLAY-possess-a-lot.

#6 [Q] — **Is sex drawn for every life, as the site does?** ·
**Decision: no, not in this campaign. No species in the tree carries a sex
model; BIO-3 (mating systems) and SOC-2 (gender arrangements) are open
registry rows and the prerequisite. The story names the person and uses the
name thereafter; where a pronoun is unavoidable the prose is restructured to
avoid one rather than inventing a sex the world does not model.** ·
Why: drawing sex without a model behind it is exactly an invented tile —
the site's own rule forbids it, and so does the project's ("an invented
figure would be worse than silence"). ·
Alternatives discarded: a coin-flip sex per lot (an authored 50/50 with no
model — and for a species whose reproduction BIO-11 or BIO-16 might make
sexless, wrong outright). ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (negation: a life with no sex at all —
which is what several registry rows already describe as species variants;
so the *absence* is a legal world-state, not a gap in the lens), 0
overturns. ·
Capture: spec §5 (slot table, `sex` row marked SILENT by design); G3 flagged
as a fidelity item.

#7 [Q] — **Which surface is canonical — the browser exhibit or the text?** ·
**Decision: the text. `windows/lot`'s prose rendering and its
`lot/life/v1` JSON are the artifacts the sim guarantees byte-for-byte; the
committed gallery page is text; the browser exhibit consumes the JSON
through the wasm ABI and is free (decision 0022). The four-stage reveal, the
log/linear graph switch, the pick-your-own year and place are client
theatre over the same payload.** ·
Why: "sim first, game as lens"; the determinism boundary is the repo
boundary up to the wasm ABI (0055), and the golden smoke test that compares
wasm output to the native CLI for the same seed is the existing instrument —
a `lot/life/v1` payload for `(42, 0)` from `hw_lot(0)` must equal `hornvale
lot --seed 42 --index 0 --json` byte for byte, and that is the exhibit's
gate. ·
Alternatives discarded: an exhibit that draws its own life client-side from
the odds payload (then two readers of one world could disagree about the
same permalink). ·
ideonomy passes / overturns: 1 pass (distribution: the site's client also
holds state — the picked year and place ride "in the code as a gazetteer row
index" because "a chosen place cannot come from a seed"; so a *picked* lot
needs a second key form, `<seed>/<index>?year=Y&site=V`, which is the
override the exhibit's pick mode produces and the CLI accepts as
`--year`/`--site`), 0 overturns. ·
Capture: spec §6, §7.

## Ideonomy — the G1 organon

Tuple picked: operators *organon-construction* + *abstraction-lift*; organon
*dictionary*; dimension prompts *autonomy*, *cardinality*, *distribution*.

**Lifted shape.** Strip the goblins and the ledger and the site is: *an
importance sample from a space-time volume, then a slice of a fact store
along the sample's worldline, rendered with provenance*. Recognizable
elsewhere as a Monte Carlo path (sample by weight, gather along it), as a
cohort life table read one member at a time, and — the one the spec leans on
— as `git blame` over a biography: every sentence names the commit that
wrote it. Most usefully it is the **dual of the census**: the census
aggregates the ledger into columns over many worlds; the lot disaggregates
one world into one row. Same instrument family, opposite direction, and the
lot's silences measure a resolution the census cannot see — whether the
ledger can say anything about an *individual* at all.

**Dictionary of The Lot.**

```
term            definition (genus; differentia)
--------------  -----------------------------------------------------------
lot             an individual life (genus) drawn from a world's person-
                years by a reader-supplied index, told from committed
                facts, never committed (differentia)
founder         an individual life the WORLD remembers: committed by
                domains/person, named at genesis, one per notable founding
roster member   an individual body derived in the standing present by
                windows/vessel (derive_npcs), possessable, uncommitted
possessed body  the roster member the reader is currently wearing
person-years    the integral of a community's live population over its
                tenure; the lot's draw weight; committed as occ-person-years
birth weight    person-years x births-per-person-year (1/e0)
worldline       the (site, birth year, death year) segment a lot occupies;
                may span two occupations when the first ends by migration
slot            a named question the story asks of the ledger ("what tongue",
                "what dwelling", "what fate did the community meet")
silence         a slot no committed fact answers; counted, never filled
source          the (entity, predicate) a sentence read; the story's blame
odds            the demographic distribution at (people, site, year): e0,
                died-before-maturity, causes — the Life stage
life course     the sequence of drawn events: birth, maturity, community
                fate, death (or "alive at now")
hazard          the Siler curve + the community's discrete fate
souls-ever      sum over occupations of person-years x birth rate: how many
                lives a world has held; the hero number
census dual     the lot disaggregates what the census aggregates
```

Overlap check: *lot* vs *roster member* — both derived, uncommitted,
deterministic in `(world, key)`; the distinction is time: a roster member
exists in the present with a body and a position, a lot is a whole life
across the bake and is usually dead. *Founder* is the only one the world
itself commits, and the lens should say so when a lot's community founder
turns up in its descent (`descent::kinship`). Circularity check: *birth
weight* → *hazard* → *e₀* → *birth weight* is grounded, not circular, because
the hazard's scale comes from allometric lifespan, an external input.

**Axes.** *Autonomy* — the world drives founders, the reader drives lots;
the substitution "let the world draw its own lots" is `domains/person`, and
already exists. *Cardinality* — one lot is an anecdote (memory: one world is
an anecdote), N lots are a sample, and the gallery (few) and a lab study
(many) are different deliverables over one function; the hero number is the
finite cardinality of the thing sampled. *Distribution* — a life's facts are
concentrated in one occupation record and distributed across every domain
that committed a fact at that site in that span (climate, sky events,
hazards, religion, language, culture, tribute); the lens is a **join across
the ledger keyed on space-time**, which is why it needs to be a window and
not a domain.

**Enrichments adopted (no overturn):** the census-dual framing (spec §2);
`souls-ever` as a committed census column (spec §8); the four-kinds-of-
individual dictionary, which the book chapter will carry; the picked-lot
key form (#7).
